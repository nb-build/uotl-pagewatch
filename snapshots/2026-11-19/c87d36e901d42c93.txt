Certificial’s Smart COI™ Network connects all parties in one platform.

Certificates of Insurance (COIs) are essential documents used by various stakeholders, all with unique purposes. Certificial’s SmartCOI™ Network ensures that all parties have access to the most current and accurate information, in real-time.

SmartCOIs™ work like this: Insurance Agencies can integrate with Certificial, automatically update COIs whenever there are changes to coverage, such as policy limits and endorsements, eliminating the need for manual updates, emails, or phone calls. SmartCOIs™ provide the latest information, and keeping the Agent, Insured, and Requestor up-to-date with the latest policy information and COI's.

The Complete Guide to Schedule-Level Insurance Data

Schedule-level insurance data is the asset-specific detail inside an insurance policy's schedules, including vehicle VINs, equipment serial numbers, and covered property locations, and most certificates of insurance leave it off entirely. Insurance agents generally omit VINs and equipment details from COIs because schedules change daily as vendors add, remove, or transfer assets, which creates legal and errors-and-omissions exposure. According to legal analysis from Kaufman Dolowich LLP (2025), if an unlisted vehicle causes an accident, the carrier can deny coverage and the contractor's own insurer is forced to defend through trial. Real-time schedule-level tracking closes that gap.

What Is An Additional Insured?

An additional insured is a person or organization protected under another party's insurance policy in addition to the primary insured, typically added by endorsement at the request of a counterparty in a contract. The status is most commonly required in construction, transportation, healthcare, and entertainment contracts, where one party needs coverage extended from another party's general liability or auto policy to protect against claims arising from the relationship. Additional insured status is established by an endorsement document attached to the underlying policy, and the list of additional insureds is typically shown in the description of operations section on a certificate of insurance.
