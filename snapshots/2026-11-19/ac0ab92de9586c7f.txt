7 Best Fitness Studio Software Platforms (2026)

Liz Childers

|

Published

June 19, 2026

TL;DR

PushPress is our top pick for the best fitness studio software in 2026. It runs the full operational stack (billing, scheduling, member management, and a member app) across yoga, pilates, studio boxing, functional fitness, and martial arts, with a genuinely free plan, transparent pricing, and built-in CRM and marketing through PushPress Grow.

Choosing software is one of the highest-stakes decisions a studio owner makes. The platform you pick runs your billing, owns your member data, powers your booking experience, and quietly shapes how much time you spend on admin versus coaching. Switch too often and you burn weeks on migrations; pick wrong and you pay for it every single month.

We compared the seven fitness studio platforms operators ask about most in 2026, looking at pricing, ease of use, core features, support, and how well each one actually fits a studio. Here's how they stack up, starting with what to look for.

What to Look For in Fitness Studio Software

Most platforms can schedule a class and charge a card. The differences that matter show up in the details: the fees buried below the sticker price, how fast support answers when billing breaks, and whether the tool was built for a studio or retrofitted from a salon booking app.

Our Evaluation Criteria

Pricing transparency & total cost: The monthly plan is only part of the bill. Payment processing rates, per-member fees, and add-on modules often double the real cost. We weighted platforms that publish clear pricing and avoid surprise escalation.

Core features: Member management, billing, class scheduling, and a member-facing app are table stakes. We looked at how well each platform handles all four without forcing extra subscriptions.

Ease of use: Software you fight with is software your front desk avoids. Setup time and day-to-day friction matter as much as the feature list.

Customer support: When a payment fails or a member can't book, response time is everything. Slow, scripted support is the most common complaint across this entire category.

Studio-specific fit: General-purpose booking tools and CrossFit-only platforms both struggle when your studio offers a mix of classes, appointments, and memberships.

2. Mindbody

Mindbody is the most recognized name in the category and remains a serious option for large studios and chains that want marketplace exposure. Its consumer app puts your studio in front of people actively searching for classes nearby, and its feature depth covers nearly every scenario a big operation could need.

Comparing it with PushPress directly? See PushPress vs Mindbody: published prices, contract terms, and who should stay on Mindbody.

That reach comes at a cost. Mindbody publishes one price, $79 a month per location for Starter, and quotes Accelerate and Ultimate on a sales call. When the Mindbody app sends you a new client, Mindbody takes 20% of that first purchase, capped at $30, on top of standard processing. Reviewers consistently flag price escalation over time, a steep learning curve, and slow support as the trade-offs of going with the incumbent.

For a small or mid-sized studio that values simplicity and control, Mindbody often means paying for features and complexity you'll never use. For a multi-location business with staff dedicated to managing the tech, it can still make sense.

Pros:

Massive consumer marketplace for discovery

Deep feature set for complex, multi-location operations

Strong brand recognition with members

Cons:

Only the entry tier has a published price, plus a fee on new clients from the marketplace

Frequent complaints about price increases and contract exits

Slow support and a complex learning curve

Pricing: From $79/mo (Starter); Accelerate and Ultimate quoted on a sales call. Processing extra, plus 20% (capped at $30) on a new marketplace client's first purchase.

3. TeamUp

TeamUp has earned some of the most loyal reviews in the category by doing one thing extremely well: making group-class management simple and predictable. Its all-features-included model and price cap mean costs stay flat as you grow, which operators love after getting burned by per-member pricing elsewhere.

Scheduling, payments, CRM, and reporting are all clean and easy to use, and TeamUp's support reputation is excellent. The trade-off is that it's deliberately focused, so studios wanting a heavily branded member app or deep marketing automation may find it lighter than the bigger platforms.

For a group-fitness operator who values transparency and a frustration-free daily experience, TeamUp is a standout.

Pros:

Transparent, capped pricing with all features included

Consistently high review scores and strong support

Streamlined scheduling and invoicing

Cons:

Lighter on branded-app and advanced marketing features

Best suited to class-based models specifically

Pricing: ~$99–$119/mo depending on member count; 14-day free trial.

4. Zen Planner

Zen Planner is purpose-built for small-to-mid-size fitness businesses like martial arts schools, boutique gyms, and yoga studios, and is well regarded for ease of use and helpful support. Its membership management, scheduling, and billing core covers the fundamentals reliably, and its reporting earns praise.

The friction shows up in pricing. The base plan ($99/mo) handles member management, scheduling, and billing, but CRM (Engage) and a website are paid add-ons, pushing a full stack (their Ultimate bundle) to $348–$538/month depending on active members. Reviewers also cite high merchant processing fees and an interface that feels dated.

For membership-driven studios, especially martial arts, that want a proven, supportive platform and don't mind the add-on math, Zen Planner is a solid pick.

Pros:

Purpose-built for membership and martial-arts studios

Easy to use with strong reporting

Well-regarded customer support

Cons:

CRM and website cost extra, raising true total cost

Complaints about high processing fees

Interface feels dated to some users

Pricing: $99/mo (Studio) · $198/mo (Essentials) · $348/mo (Ultimate); add-ons push full stack higher.

5. Glofox

Glofox (now ABC Glofox, part of ABC Fitness since 2022) is built for boutique studios and multi-location fitness brands. Its business-type pages cover yoga, Pilates, cycling, boxing and martial arts, and it does a few things most studio software doesn't: floor and bike maps so members can pick their exact spot, a ClassPass integration, 17+ languages and multi-currency billing.

The catch is seeing the price. Glofox publishes one number, plans starting at $99 a month, and lists four plans (Essential, Boost, Elite and Enterprise) with features but no prices. Digital waivers, retail and online lead forms start on Boost, the custom branded app is an add-on below Elite, and processing rates aren't published. Here's what the $99 actually covers.

For a cycling studio where members book a specific bike, or a franchise running in more than one country, Glofox is a strong fit. Get every plan and rate quoted in writing before you sign.

Pros:

Spot and bike booking with visual floor maps

ClassPass integration

Multi-language, multi-currency and franchise tools

Staff app and payroll on the entry plan

Cons:

Only a starting price is published; every plan is quoted

Waivers, retail and lead forms start on the second plan, Boost

Branded app is an add-on below Elite

Processing rates aren't published

Pricing: From $99/mo per Glofox's plans page; each plan is quoted via sales. Full breakdown.

6. Wodify

Wodify is purpose-built for CrossFit and functional fitness, and it's the best on this list at workout tracking: logs, personal records, and leaderboards that members of a box genuinely engage with. It also bundles billing, waivers, and member communication, and earns high review scores (4.7/5) from its core audience.

Its specialization is also its ceiling. For studios offering yoga, Pilates, personal training, or a general mix, Wodify's CrossFit-first design becomes a constraint. Pricing starts at $79/month but typically layers on per-member fees that grow with your roster, and less technical admins report a learning curve.

If you run a functional fitness box and care most about workout data, Wodify is excellent. For a broader studio, it's a narrow fit.

Pros:

Standout workout tracking and leaderboards

Built specifically for CrossFit/functional fitness

High review scores from its target users

Cons:

Specialization limits non-CrossFit studios

Per-member fees scale costs as you grow

Steeper learning curve for some admins

Pricing: From $79/mo, plus per-member fees.

7. Walla

Walla is built only for boutique fitness studios, and it looks the part. Two things stand out: members can pick their exact spot in class with Book-a-Spot on every plan, and Walla's AI agents can answer texts, calls and website visitors around the clock.

Comparing it with PushPress directly? See PushPress vs Walla: what each plan costs, what processing really costs a yoga studio, and who should pick Walla.

The catch is seeing the price. Walla publishes one number, plans starting at $299 a month per location, and every plan card says "Let's talk." Two-way texting, the branded app and on-demand video courses start on Pro. The Concierge Agent and Walla Voice are included only on Scale and sold as add-ons below it, with no published price. Processing rates are in Walla's help center: 2.9% + $0.30 on cards and 1.8% on ACH, and Stripe's payout fee (0.25% + $0.25 per payout) is passed on to the studio.

For a boutique studio that wants AI texting, spot booking and on-demand video in one polished app, and is comfortable with a quoted price, Walla is a strong fit. Get every plan and add-on quoted in writing before you sign.

Pros:

Book-a-Spot on every plan

AI agents for texts, calls and website visitors

On-demand video courses from Pro up

Polished, boutique-only design

Processing rates published in its help center

Cons:

Only a starting price is published; every plan is quoted

Texting, branded app and video start on Pro

Main AI agents are Scale-only or paid add-ons

Stripe payout fee is passed on to the studio

Few reviews so far (16 on G2, 25 on Capterra)

Pricing: From $299/mo per location; each plan is quoted via sales. Cards 2.9% + $0.30, ACH 1.8%. Full comparison.

How We Ranked These Platforms

We weighted five factors: pricing transparency and total cost of ownership (including processing fees and add-ons), core feature completeness, ease of use, customer support quality, and how well each platform fits an actual fitness studio versus a general booking tool. We cross-referenced public review aggregates from G2 and Capterra with documented operator complaints from Reddit and review sites, and verified pricing against each vendor's current published information. No vendor paid for placement. Though PushPress publishes this comparison, we've represented competitor strengths honestly.

PushPress earns the top spot because it solves the two problems that dog this entire category: cost surprises and growth. The $0 Free plan lets you start without a software bill, transparent published pricing means no sales-call guessing games, and processing rates that improve as you scale reward growth instead of penalizing it.

More importantly, PushPress is the only platform here that treats marketing and retention as core to running a studio, not a bolt-on. With Grow handling lead capture and follow-up, Train powering programming and workout delivery, and a branded app for members, you get a stack that runs the business and helps it grow, backed by the responsive support that incumbents struggle to match.

For the broad middle of studios that want to run lean today and grow without re-platforming tomorrow, PushPress is the clear winner.
