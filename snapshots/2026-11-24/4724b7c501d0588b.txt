Cyrious Control alternatives for sign and print shops

If you run Cyrious Control and you have started looking around, you are
almost certainly weighing four names: CoreBridge, shopVOX, Ordant and
Printcore. Here is what each one actually costs, what moving your data really
involves, and the cases where staying on Cyrious is the right call.

Disclosure and sourcing. Printcore makes one of the products on this list, so read
the recommendation with that in mind. Everything factual here comes from the vendors' own
websites and documentation rather than from review-site summaries, and every source is
linked at the bottom of the page. Prices were read directly from each vendor's pricing page
on 12 August 2026; software pricing changes, so check before you budget.

Why shops start looking

Cyrious Software was founded in 1996 — by the owners of a sign and graphics company,
which is exactly why Control fits the trade so well. It is a deep, mature product, and the
shops that love it are not wrong to. But three structural facts drive most of the searching:

It is Windows software on your server. Control runs as a Windows application against
a Microsoft SQL Server database, which Cyrious's own wiki confirms. Cyrious does document a
hosted option, but its documentation describes signing in to a Cyrious Cloud server with a
domain\username — a Remote Desktop session, not a browser-native
application. If you want quoting from a phone in a customer's parking lot or a tablet at the
laminator, that architecture fights you.

You cannot see what it costs. There is no pricing page; cyrious.com/pricing
returns a 404, and the Control page asks you to call for a quote. The single published figure
on the site is $199 per month for the Ecommerce add-on covering three storefronts. That is a
legitimate way to sell software, but it makes a shortlist harder to build, and it is a sharp
contrast with the three alternatives below, all of which publish their tiers openly.

The public signals have gone quiet. The most recent post on the Cyrious blog is dated
8 December 2016. The most recent release documented on the Cyrious wiki is version
6.1.2406.2501, dated 25 June 2024 — that is the latest documented release, which
is not the same thing as the latest release, since a wiki can simply go unmaintained. On the
public review sites the footprint is small: as of 12 August 2026 the "Cyrious" listing shows 2.8/5
from 5 reviews on Capterra and 3.8/5 from 11 reviews on G2. Those are small samples and worth
reading individually rather than treating as a score.

When you should stay on Cyrious

Three cases, and they are common enough to say plainly. First, if
you have years of tuned cost templates that produce prices you trust, that accumulated
judgement is a real asset and rebuilding it is the largest cost of any switch. Second, if
your business runs on custom Crystal Reports, Control's reporting depth is genuinely hard to
match. Third, if your team knows the
software cold and your margins are healthy, the disruption may simply cost more than the
benefit. "Old" is not the same as "wrong for you." Switch because something specific is
broken, not because the interface looks dated.

What migrating off Cyrious actually involves

This is the part every vendor is vague about, so here is the honest version. The good news
is better than most shops expect: your data is reachable. Cyrious's own wiki states that
Control uses Microsoft SQL Server as its database engine, and its SQL Bridge documentation
explicitly sanctions reading from that database directly, recommending the
with (nolock) hint on select statements so your reads do not lock production
tables. Cyrious's FAQ also states that you hold your data locally whether you purchase or
subscribe. In practice that means a competent SQL person can pull your history out.

The bad news is what is missing around that. There is no one-click export, no published
schema, and no official migration tool, so someone has to map the tables by inspection before
anything moves. Expect to spend more time on discovery than on the transfer itself.

A realistic order of operations

You don’t need a big-bang cutover. With Printcore you set up with the in-app Setup Guide and start quoting during the trial,
so the steps below happen while both systems run side by side for a few weeks.

Customers and contacts first. The cleanest data you own, and the fastest win. It
also surfaces duplicate-record problems while they are still cheap to fix.

Your product and material catalog. Slower, because this is where naming
inconsistencies from a decade of quick fixes come to light.

Open orders and work in progress. Many shops choose to key these in by hand rather
than migrate them — there are rarely enough to justify the scripting, and the manual
pass doubles as staff training.

Historical order headers. Import for reporting and reorder lookup. Full line-item
history is often not worth the effort; decide deliberately rather than by default.

Pricing rules: rebuild, do not migrate. Formulas do not port between data models.
Take the opportunity to check them against your actual material costs, which have almost
certainly moved since the templates were written.

Reports: rebuild last. List the reports you genuinely read each week. It is
usually far shorter than the list of reports that exist.

Run both systems side by side for a few weeks before you cancel anything.
Quote in both, compare the numbers, and switch off once they match and your team has stopped
reaching for Control.

Questions to ask every vendor on your shortlist

What is the total first-year cost, including onboarding, data migration and every module
I need? Ask for one number, in writing.

Are users included, or charged per seat? At my headcount, what does that make the real
monthly figure?

Can I export my own data on demand, in bulk, without asking you — and in what
format?

Who rebuilds my pricing formulas, and is that time included or billed?

What does support cost after onboarding, and is a bug fix billable?

Show me the quoting flow for my most complicated recurring job, live, using my numbers.

That last one is the whole evaluation, honestly. Demos are built around the jobs the software
handles well. Bring the job that gives you trouble.

Frequently asked questions

Does Cyrious publish pricing for Cyrious Control?

No. The Cyrious Control product page asks you to call for a quote, and cyrious.com/pricing returns a 404. The only figure published anywhere on the site is $199 per month for the Ecommerce add-on covering three storefronts. Every alternative on this page publishes its rates openly, which makes a like-for-like budget comparison much faster.

Can I get my data out of Cyrious Control?

Yes, though not with a single button. Cyrious's own wiki states that Control uses Microsoft SQL Server as its database engine, and its SQL Bridge page documents reading from that database directly, recommending the with (nolock) hint on select statements. A competent SQL person can extract customers, contacts, orders, line items and payment history. There is no published schema and no official export tool, so budget time for someone to map the tables.

Is Cyrious Control cloud software?

Not in the way most shops now mean it. Control is a Windows application. Cyrious's documentation describes its hosted option as connecting to a Cyrious Cloud server and signing in with a domain\username — a Remote Desktop Web Access session, not a browser-native application. That distinction matters for tablets on the shop floor, for phones, and for anyone quoting from outside the building.

Which Cyrious Control alternative is the closest match?

CoreBridge Software is the most like-for-like: same industry focus, comparable breadth across estimating, production and accounting, and it is a common destination for shops that have moved off Cyrious. Printcore is a different bet — less legacy depth, but AI quoting and scheduling built into the core rather than bolted on.

Will my Cyrious pricing formulas transfer to a new system?

No, and any vendor who tells you otherwise is guessing. Tuned cost templates, markup ladders and Crystal Reports layouts are specific to Control's data model and get rebuilt rather than migrated. Treat that as the real cost of a switch. The upside is that many shops discover several years of drift in those formulas once they have to state them explicitly again.

How long does switching from Cyrious Control take?

You don't have to switch off Control on day one. With Printcore, you start with a free 20-minute demo, then set up your shop yourself during a 2-week trial with the in-app Setup Guide, with a 30-minute check-in call after the first week. Then run both systems side by side for a few weeks: quote in both, compare the numbers, and let your team get comfortable. Customers, contacts and open orders come across during that window, and pricing rules get rebuilt in Printcore rather than migrated. Turn Control off once the numbers match and nobody is reaching for the old system.
