7 Insurance Comparative Raters for 2026 [Decision Guide]

Sep 2, 2026

A comparative rater is the quoting layer that lets an independent agent enter a risk once and return premiums from many appointed carriers instead of logging into each portal. Insurance comparative rater software is not an agency management system, not a CRM, and not a commercial submission portal — it is the messenger that translates one data entry into many carrier rating APIs and shows the results side by side.

Independent agency commercial P&C share: 87% according to Big I (2024). Most commercial premium still flows through independents, so the rater decision is a production decision, not a software hobby. This guide compares seven named engines — EZLynx, TurboRater, Vertafore PL Rating, Applied Rater, QuoteRush, Tarmika, and Semsee — and separates personal-lines desks from commercial desks.

TL;DR: pick a personal-lines engine that actually rates your appointed auto and home carriers, then add a commercial engine if you write BOP, workers comp, and GL with any regularity. Do not expect EZLynx or TurboRater to replace a commercial rater. Do not buy a 14-vendor directory when a seven-name shortlist is the evaluation you can finish.

Who this is for

This page is for independent agencies that already hold carrier appointments, already run an AMS, and lose hours re-keying the same driver, VIN, and property fields into carrier sites. It is also for mixed personal-and-commercial shops that need two engines, not one “do everything” license.

Red flags: you are a captive agent whose carrier already owns the only rating path; you write only surplus lines or highly specialized commercial risks that still require ACORD packets and underwriter judgment; you have no appointed panel, so a rater with “100+ carriers” cannot return a bindable quote.

How independent agents actually quote

According to Insurance Pro Agencies (2026), quoting ten personal-lines carriers by hand can take 2–4 hours per client, while a rater can return a comparable panel in 10–15 minutes when the data is clean. That gap is the whole category: one form, many APIs, one comparison screen.

The rater does not set the rate. Carriers do. If a premium looks wrong, the usual causes are a bad VIN, a missing household driver, the wrong coverage limits, or a carrier-specific rating factor — not a mysterious “rater algorithm.”

Insurance pages earned: 8.0% according to US Tech Automations first-party mix-config on a 12,514-page corpus. That is why this page stays on seven named raters instead of a directory dump.

EZLynx vs TurboRater vs PL Rating

EZLynx is the default personal-lines comparative rater in the independent channel: auto, home, renters, umbrella, a consumer quoting portal, and broad carrier connectivity. Insurance Pro Agencies describes EZLynx as connecting 100+ carriers and as the engine many aggregators bundle. Choose EZLynx when you want the widest personal-lines panel and you already live in, or will accept, the EZLynx AMS orbit. Skip EZLynx as your only tool if commercial volume is real — InSifter is blunt that personal-lines raters do not handle commercial well.

TurboRater, from Insurance Technologies Corporation, is the cost-conscious personal-lines alternative. It is web-based, auto-and-home focused, and often shortlisted when the agency does not want the full EZLynx platform cost. Choose TurboRater when the panel is smaller, personal lines dominate, and you will not pay for an ecosystem you will not use. Skip it when you need EZLynx-depth commercial-lite rating or a consumer portal as a lead engine.

Vertafore PL Rating is the personal-lines engine for shops already standardized on Vertafore (AMS360, AgencyZoom, related rating). Choose PL Rating when the AMS is Vertafore and you want rating next to the policy record instead of a second login. Skip it when your AMS is Applied Epic or HawkSoft and you would be forcing a Vertafore-only quoting layer.

Applied Rater (formerly Semcat) is the Applied Epic-family rater: personal lines plus light commercial, strongest where Applied’s carrier relationships are deep. Pair it with Applied Epic alternatives for insurance agencies only if you are also reconsidering the AMS, not because the rater failed a single carrier.

QuoteRush (also styled QuoteRUSH in commercial-rater roundups) is the other personal-lines name agencies put next to EZLynx when they want comparative auto/home without defaulting to the EZLynx AMS. Treat it as a personal-lines engine in the two-rater stack, not as a Tarmika replacement.

Commercial lines: Tarmika vs Semsee

According to InSifter (June 2026), commercial raters are a separate category with smaller carrier counts — typically 25–50 versus 100–180 for personal lines — because commercial questions and applications fragment by carrier. Tarmika is the default small-commercial comparative rater (BOP, workers comp, GL, commercial auto, cyber, professional), with 40+ carriers in that roundup, and it has been part of Applied Systems since 2021. Semsee is the low-volume option: pay per quote, no monthly seat tax, 35+ carriers in the same roundup.

The two-rater reality is the operating model: a personal-lines engine (EZLynx, TurboRater, or Vertafore PL Rating) plus a commercial engine (Tarmika or Semsee). InSifter’s combined-stack illustration is roughly $300–500 per user per month as an agent-reported range, not a public vendor list price — confirm both invoices in writing.

US P&C direct written premiums reached $1.07T in 2024 according to the Insurance Information Institute 2025 Fact Book, which is why a mixed agency cannot treat commercial quoting as a side quest.

Per-vendor profiles

EZLynx

Best fit: independent personal-lines volume, aggregators that already include EZLynx, agencies that want a consumer quoting portal on the website. Limitations: commercial is limited; you will still run Tarmika or Semsee for real small-commercial. Implementation: plan for AMS data mapping, carrier appointments, and producer training on data quality (VIN, drivers, roof age). Evidence: Insurance Pro Agencies’ 2026 rater guide and EZLynx’s own rating-engine positioning.

TurboRater

Best fit: personal auto/home shops that want comparative rating without buying the EZLynx platform. Limitations: narrower line set; not a commercial engine. Implementation: shorter than an AMS swap, still requires carrier-by-carrier connectivity checks. Evidence: Insurance Pro Agencies’ TurboRater section (2026).

Vertafore PL Rating

Best fit: AMS360 / Vertafore-standardized agencies quoting personal lines next to the policy file. Limitations: weak choice if Applied Epic is the system of record. Implementation: inherits the Vertafore stack; do not dual-run PL Rating and EZLynx without a written data-flow. Evidence: Vertafore product family plus Insurance Pro Agencies’ AMS-integration criterion.

Applied Rater

Best fit: Applied Epic agencies that want rating inside the Epic workflow. Limitations: light commercial is not Tarmika; complex accounts still go to ACORD 125 and underwriters. Implementation: Epic-first. If Epic itself is in question, read the Applied Epic alternatives comparison before you add another Applied SKU. Evidence: Insurance Pro Agencies on Applied Rater (formerly Semcat).

QuoteRush

Best fit: personal-lines comparative quoting when EZLynx is not the default AMS choice. Limitations: not a commercial rater; confirm your appointed carriers before you celebrate the logo count. Implementation: treat it as the personal-lines half of a two-rater desk. Evidence: InSifter’s personal-lines naming of QuoteRUSH alongside EZLynx and PL Rating (June 2026).

Tarmika

Best fit: agencies writing meaningful small-commercial volume (BOP, WC, GL) who want one submission to many commercial carriers. Limitations: not auto/home; Applied ownership is a plus for Epic shops and a question for Vertafore shops until you test the bridge. Implementation: map AMS, confirm the 40-carrier-class panel against your appointments, train CSRs on commercial questions that personal-lines screens omit. Evidence: InSifter commercial-rater guide (June 2026).

Semsee

Best fit: low commercial quote volume where a subscription would sit idle; pay when you quote. Limitations: at higher monthly quote counts a flat Tarmika seat can become cheaper per quote — InSifter’s crossover discussion is around 20–30 commercial quotes per month as a planning band, not a guarantee. Implementation: lightest commercial stand-up on this list. Evidence: InSifter Semsee profile (June 2026).

Worked example: a dual-rater desk

A mixed independent with 6 producers, 22 personal-lines quotes on a typical Monday, and 9 small-commercial BOP/GL attempts the same week cannot run one engine. Personal-lines files move through EZLynx in the 10–15 minute band Insurance Pro Agencies cites, while the same 9 commercial accounts would still take 90 minutes each across three carrier portals if the desk skipped Tarmika — InSifter’s 90-to-15-minute BOP contrast. After a bind, staff write the policy identifiers back into Applied Epic on Policy.PolicyNumber so the rater output and the AMS record stay the same client. US Tech Automations can sit above that desk as the workflow that copies a completed personal-lines quote into the commercial rater only when a BOP indicator is present, then opens a human review queue when a carrier returns refer-to-company instead of a bindable premium.

Common quoting mistakes

Do not buy carrier count. Buy appointed-carrier hit rate. Do not quote state-minimum auto to win a screenshot, then re-quote real limits after the client says yes. Do not skip household drivers. Do not treat a comparative rater as an AMS — policy, commission, and certificate work still lives in agency management software. Do not put commercial ACORD 125 work into EZLynx and call it done.

DIY raters in Zapier, Make, or n8n

The real alternative to a packaged rater is not “do nothing.” It is stitching carrier portals, a spreadsheet, and a no-code tool. Zapier, Make, and n8n can hold run histories, retries, error branches, and audit logs when you design them that way. You still own observability, idempotency (one quote request must not create three carrier submissions), escalation when an API 500s, access control on unencrypted application data, retention of quotes, and maintenance when a carrier changes a field. A proposed US Tech Automations design would take an EZLynx or TurboRater completion event, require a producer to confirm the VIN and driver list, then either open Tarmika for commercial or write Policy.PolicyNumber into the AMS — with a human bind step that no workflow is allowed to skip.

When NOT to use US Tech Automations

Skip the orchestration layer when a single rater plus the AMS already covers the only workflow you run — for example a personal-lines-only shop that lives entirely in EZLynx and never dual-keys. Skip it when the commercial book is a handful of brokered surplus files that still need underwriter narrative, not a BOP rater. Skip it when you have no one who will own quote-data quality; automating a bad VIN just scales wrong premiums.

FAQ

What is the best comparative rater for independent agents?

The best comparative rater for independent agents is the engine that rates your appointed personal-lines panel; EZLynx is the usual default, with TurboRater and Vertafore PL Rating as stack-fit alternatives, and Tarmika or Semsee added for commercial.

Is EZLynx better than TurboRater?

EZLynx is broader (more lines, consumer portal, aggregator bundling); TurboRater is the lighter personal-lines alternative — pick on appointed-carrier hit rate and AMS family, not on a universal score.

Can I use EZLynx as commercial insurance quoting software?

Not as your commercial engine: InSifter’s June 2026 commercial-rater guide says personal-lines raters do not quote commercial well, and mixed agencies run two raters.

How much does insurance quoting software cost in 2026?

Public bindable list prices were not on the vendor pages checked 2026-09-02; request a dated per-seat or per-quote quote and ignore unofficial “starts around” blogs as order-form numbers.

Who should choose Tarmika instead of Semsee?

Choose Tarmika when small-commercial quote volume is steady enough that a subscription beats per-quote fees and you want the larger Applied-owned carrier panel; choose Semsee when commercial quoting is occasional.

Do I still need an AMS if I buy a rater?

Yes. The rater quotes; the AMS is the policy system of record — see best insurance agency management software for that layer.

Key Takeaways

Independents still control 87% of commercial P&C share, so quoting speed is a revenue system, not an IT accessory.

Personal-lines raters (EZLynx, TurboRater, PL Rating, Applied Rater, QuoteRush) and commercial raters (Tarmika, Semsee) are different products; mixed books run both.

Score appointed-carrier hit rate at 25% of the decision; ignore logo-wall carrier counts.

Print no vendor dollar from a blog; collect dated quotes on 2026-09-02 terms.

Orchestration only earns its keep when a quote must move between rater, AMS, and a human bind step without dual-keying.
