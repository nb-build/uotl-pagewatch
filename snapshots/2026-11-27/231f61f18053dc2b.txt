What is a cloud phone system?

A cloud phone system is a business phone service that runs over the internet from the provider's data centers instead of on PBX hardware in your office, so your team can make and take calls on a desk phone, laptop or mobile app. Cloud VoIP, hosted VoIP and cloud PBX are other names for the same thing. The modern cloud phone stack covers inbound routing, outbound dialing, SMS, voicemail, and usually a video or team chat layer bundled as UCaaS (unified communications as a service).

Several tools on this list now include some form of AI call handling, which changes what a cloud phone system can do when no one is free to answer.

How a cloud phone system works

The provider hosts the call control software, connects it to phone carriers, and gives you an admin portal. When someone dials your number, the call reaches the provider, which applies your routing rules (business hours, auto attendant, ring groups, queues) and delivers the call over the internet to an app or IP desk phone. Adding a user means assigning a license and a number in the portal.

Cloud phone system vs VoIP vs hosted PBX

VoIP is the technology that carries voice over an IP network. A cloud phone system is the full product built on it: numbers, routing, voicemail, texting, apps, analytics and admin. Hosted PBX and cloud PBX are older names for a PBX run by the provider. Cloud telephony is the broader term, which also covers SIP trunks and voice APIs that developers build on.

How to choose a cloud phone system

Start with the problem you are solving, then check the plan tier that solves it, then price that tier for your headcount.

Routing calls or answering them. If your team needs internal calling, video meetings and CRM-connected calling, a UCaaS platform like RingCentral, Nextiva or Dialpad fits. If your bottleneck is unanswered calls, after-hours coverage or volume beyond your team's capacity, an AI voice agent platform like Retell AI solves a different problem.

Texting volume. Count the texts each user sends in a month and compare it with the plan allowance, not the word "SMS" on the feature list.

Integrations. Confirm your CRM is supported on the tier you plan to buy. In most lineups here that is the second tier.

International calling. List the countries you call. 8x8 X4 covers 48 countries and GoTo Connect lists free calls to 50, while Nextiva and Zoom Phone meter international calls.

Compliance. If you handle patient data, confirm in writing that the vendor will sign a business associate agreement on your tier.

Support hours. Check which plan includes phone support. Nextiva and Dialpad both start 24/7 phone or customer support on their $25 tier.

Then run a trial on your own internet connection and ask for a sample invoice that includes taxes and fees.

How to switch to a cloud phone system

List what you use today. Count users, numbers, locations, ring groups, auto attendant menus and any fax or analog lines.

Test your network. Run a trial on the connection your team will use, including home offices.

Pick the plan tier. Match texting volume, CRM and support needs to the tier that includes them.

Port your numbers. Keep the old service active until the port completes. FCC rules require carriers to complete a simple port within one business day unless the new provider or the customer asks for longer. The rule covers simple ports only, so ask the new provider whether your account qualifies and for a firm date.

How does SIP trunking connect an AI voice agent to a cloud phone system?

SIP trunking is a way to carry business phone calls over the internet between a carrier, a cloud phone system and other services such as AI voice agents.

It lets you keep your numbers and your cloud phone system while an AI agent answers some or all of your calls. Your team keeps its extensions, apps and desk phones.

Retell AI connects over SIP to Twilio, Vonage, Telnyx or any SIP carrier. Retell does not charge for telephony when you use your own SIP trunk, according to its pricing page.

A typical setup takes three steps:

Route your main line, an overflow queue or after-hours calls from your cloud phone system to the agent.

Connect the agent to your calendar and CRM through function calling and webhooks.

Use warm transfer to send live calls back to an extension or queue with a summary first.

Some cloud phone systems also support bring your own carrier. Zoom, for example, offers a Zoom Phone Pro license for Bring-Your-Own-Carrier setups.

What do businesses use a cloud phone system for?

24/7 after-hours call answering: Callers who reach voicemail often hang up without leaving a message. An AI voice agent layered onto your cloud phone system as an AI answering service can book those callers instead of sending them to voicemail. See how overflow and after-hours routing works.

Appointment booking with live calendar sync: Healthcare, home services, and legal practices lose appointments to phone tag. Real-time function calling to Cal.com, Google Calendar, or an EHR lets a voice agent book appointments during the call instead of promising a callback.

Replacing legacy IVR menus: "Press 1 for billing" routes calls but does not handle them. A modern AI IVR understands intent, holds a conversation, and routes based on what the caller needs.

Outbound sales dialing: A seat-based phone system places one call per user at a time. Sales dialers such as the power and parallel modes on CloudTalk queue the next call for the rep, and AI voice agents can run outbound campaigns without a rep on each line.

Inbound lead qualification before human handoff: Routing every inbound call to a sales rep spends time on unqualified callers. Lead qualification by a voice agent ahead of warm transfer sends qualified callers to sales with context.

Warm transfer settings in Retell. A whisper debrief briefs the person picking up without the caller hearing it.
Multi-location receptionist coverage: Small offices and multi-site service businesses often staff a receptionist at every site. A single voice agent can cover every location, route to the right person, book appointments, and forward urgent calls.

What can go wrong with a cloud phone system?

Fees that are not in the per-user price: Vendor price lists exclude taxes and regulatory fees. RingCentral lists federal universal service, E911 and compliance cost recovery fees, and CloudTalk adds an 11% cost recovery fee for US customers. Add-ons such as extra numbers, toll-free minutes and AI features come on top.

SMS limits on entry plans: RingCentral Core includes 25 SMS per user per month and Nextiva Go includes 50. CloudTalk includes none and bills per message. US business texting also needs Campaign Registry registration before messages deliver reliably, so plan for that step before launch.

AI features sold as separate subscriptions: An AI receptionist or conversation intelligence is usually priced on top of the seat. RingCentral lists AI Receptionist from $39 per month and AI Conversation Expert from $60 per user per month.

Compliance certifications that sound the same but are not: SOC 2 Type I differs materially from Type II. HIPAA BAA availability varies by plan and sometimes by add-on. Buyers in healthcare and finance need to confirm the specific certification and whether it is included on their chosen tier.

Internet dependency: Calls run over your internet connection. Weak Wi-Fi or an outage affects call quality unless you set call forwarding to mobile numbers as a fallback.

Per-seat cost at high call volume: Per-seat plans grow with headcount. If the growth is in call volume and not in staff, compare the cost of more seats with the cost of automating the calls.
