TL;DR

Kaseya doesn’t publish a price for VSA, Datto RMM or Kaseya 365 Endpoint, and kaseya.com/pricing returns a 404. It lists only a few prices, such as Kaseya 365 Ops at $129 per user per month on a one-year term.

VSA 9 reaches end of life on August 31, 2027. Kaseya names Datto RMM as the primary migration target, with VSA 10 as the alternate path, so every VSA 9 shop has a platform decision to make either way.

Since December 1, 2025 invoices, Datto RMM, Autotask and SaaS Protection bill on a Committed Minimum Quantity (CMQ): you pay your contracted minimum even when usage drops below it.

Four of the ten alternatives publish a paid rate without a sales call: NinjaOne, Atera, Syncro and ManageEngine. Action1 publishes a free tier. The other five are quote-only.

Our pick for MSPs leaving Kaseya over pricing opacity is Syncro: RMM and PSA in one platform at $129 or $179 per technician per month billed annually, with unlimited endpoints. Ranked third and disclosed below.

You can’t budget against a price you can’t see. That’s the practical problem with Kaseya in 2026, and it’s why most MSPs start looking.

Kaseya lists no price for VSA, Datto RMM or the Kaseya 365 Endpoint bundle. The VSA product URL now redirects to the Datto RMM page, and kaseya.com/pricing returns a 404.

Two dates make the decision more pressing. Kaseya moved Datto RMM, Autotask and SaaS Protection to committed-minimum billing from December 1, 2025 invoices, and it has set August 31, 2027 as the end of life for VSA 9.

This list compares ten alternatives on the two things that are hardest to find and easiest to verify: what they charge, and what they lock you into. Where a vendor doesn’t publish a price, we say so instead of estimating.

Disclosure: Syncro is our platform. We ranked it third rather than first, and we applied the same test to it as to everyone else: is the price published, and what does the commitment look like? Weight the G2 reviews above any vendor blog, including ours.

How We Evaluated These Tools

Four criteria, in this order:

Published pricing. Can an MSP read the rate on the vendor’s own site without a sales call? It’s a fair proxy for how the vendor will treat you at renewal, and it’s the test Kaseya fails.

Pricing unit and commitment. Per endpoint, per technician or per user, plus any minimum quantity or term. Per-endpoint pricing moves against you as clients grow.

Platform scope. RMM only, or RMM plus PSA, ticketing and billing? Replacing Kaseya with three tools isn’t a consolidation.

G2 rating and review volume. A 4.8 across 40 reviews and a 4.7 across 5,000 aren’t the same signal, so we show both.

Sources: vendor pricing pages and documentation, G2 listings, Kaseya’s billing and end-of-life notices, and Omdia’s Global RMM and PSA Leadership Matrix 2026. All figures were checked on November 5, 2026 and will drift. Anything we couldn’t verify from a primary source isn’t in this article.

The Filter Nobody Puts on These Lists: Who Will Tell You the Price

We checked every vendor here against one question. Can an MSP read the rate on the vendor’s own site, today, without talking to anyone?

Four can. NinjaOne publishes a per-device range in its pricing FAQ. Atera publishes per-technician tiers for both MSPs and IT departments. Syncro publishes two flat per-technician plans. ManageEngine publishes per-endpoint bands. Action1 publishes its free tier and quotes everything above it.

Five can’t. SuperOps, ConnectWise, N-able, Datto RMM and Pulseway all route to a quote. SuperOps moved to quote-only per-endpoint pricing in 2026, and N-able’s pricing URL returns a 404, the same as Kaseya’s.

This matters beyond convenience. A vendor that publishes a rate has to defend every increase against it in public. A vendor that quotes per deal prices per deal, which is why renewal is the conversation MSPs dread. If pricing opacity pushed you off Kaseya, swapping in another quote-only vendor solves nothing. For a line-by-line breakdown of what Kaseya does and doesn’t publish, see our Kaseya pricing guide.

What You’re Actually Replacing

Kaseya sells two RMM lines today: Datto RMM and VSA. VSA 10 is current, and VSA 9 gets only sustaining updates until its August 31, 2027 end of life. Kaseya’s notice names Datto RMM as the primary migration target for VSA 9 customers, with VSA 10 as the alternate path.

Inside the Kaseya 365 Endpoint bundle, the RMM component can be Datto RMM, VSA 10 or VSA 9 (current users only). It bundles Datto AV, Datto EDR with ransomware detection, third-party patching and endpoint backup, plus RocketCyber MDR on the Pro tier. None of it has a published price, and new customers start at 50 endpoints.

One more date for MSPs on Kaseya 365 User: Datto SaaS Defense support ends December 31, 2026, and the product is decommissioned on June 30, 2027. INKY has been the default email security for new Kaseya 365 User sales since January 1, 2026.

If you’re on VSA 9, a migration is coming regardless. The real question is whether it lands on another Kaseya platform or somewhere else.

The 10 Alternatives

1. NinjaOne: The Largest Review Base, With a Published Range

NinjaOne is the default answer, and unlike most large RMM vendors, it puts a price range on its pricing page. Its FAQ lists per-device pricing from $1.50 per month at 10,000 endpoints up to $3.75 at 50 or fewer, for the commercial (non-FedRAMP) instance, varying by region and products purchased. NinjaOne also says it doesn’t list full pricing publicly, so expect a quote for your exact mix.

Omdia’s July 2026 Managed BDR Leadership Matrix put NinjaOne above $500 million ARR, up 70% year over year, and NinjaOne reports more than 35,000 customers in more than 140 countries. It completed its roughly $270 million acquisition of SaaS backup vendor Dropsuite in June 2025.

What we like: A published per-device range that falls as you grow. The largest G2 review base on this list, about 4.5 times Atera’s. Strong endpoint management and patching, and a native PSA, generally available since Q1 2026.

What we don’t like: NinjaOne PSA is new, so it’s far less proven than Autotask (see our NinjaOne vs Kaseya comparison). Per-device pricing still grows with every client endpoint, the same structural problem as Kaseya. Backup and other products are priced on top.

Best for: MSPs who want the most-reviewed RMM and already have a PSA they intend to keep.

Pricing: $1.50 to $3.75 per device per month, published as a range. 14-day free trial.

G2 rating: 4.7 out of 5 across 5,840 reviews.

2. Atera: Per-Technician Pricing With a Full Published Ladder

Atera prices per technician with unlimited endpoints, which removes the endpoint-growth problem entirely. Its MSP plans are Pro at $129 per technician per month billed annually ($139 monthly), Growth at $159 ($189), Power at $209 ($249) and Superpower by quote.

Separate IT department plans run from $149 to $219 per technician per month billed annually. AI Copilot is included in every plan, and Gartner named Atera a Visionary in its 2026 Magic Quadrant for Endpoint Management Tools.

What we like: A real published rate for every MSP tier but the top one. Per-technician, unlimited-endpoint pricing. A 30-day free trial with no credit card, tied with ManageEngine for the longest evaluation window here.

What we don’t like: The entry MSP plan supports Windows only. Mac and Linux support starts at Growth, so the comparable price for a mixed fleet is $159, not $129. Atera also moved legacy accounts to current website pricing in 2026 (monthly plans from June billing, annual plans at renewals on or after July 28), so long-time customers should check what they now pay.

Best for: MSPs and lean IT teams who want per-technician pricing and can work within the tier gating.

Pricing: MSP Pro $129, Growth $159, Power $209 per technician per month billed annually. 30-day free trial, no credit card.

G2 rating: 4.6 out of 5 across 1,287 reviews.

3. Syncro: Our Pick for MSPs Leaving Over Pricing

Syncro combines RMM, PSA, ticketing, billing and Microsoft 365 management in one platform, priced per technician with unlimited endpoints on every plan. Core is $129 per technician per month billed annually ($159 monthly), and Team is $179 ($209 monthly).

That published flat rate is why it sits at number three. If not knowing what you’d pay is what pushed you off Kaseya, this is the shortest distance to knowing. Here’s why MSPs switch from Kaseya to Syncro in more detail.

The math is simple to check. Four technicians on the annual Team plan cost $716 a month whether they manage 600 endpoints (about $1.19 each) or 1,200 (about $0.60 each).

On capability, the parts that matter for a Kaseya replacement:

Patching: Windows update policies use four approval states (Approve, Defer, Reject, Manual) on a daily, weekly or monthly schedule. Policies cascade through a folder hierarchy that works like a Group Policy Object model.

Scripting: PowerShell, Batch, VBScript and Mac Bash, with Bash on the Linux agent, plus a Community Script Library.

Migration: Dedicated importers for Atera, Autotask and ConnectWise, a script path to install the Syncro agent on NinjaOne-managed devices, and a universal agent installer for everything else. Syncro also offers contract buyout options for MSPs locked into an existing agreement.

Integrations: Splashtop remote access is built in, alongside integrations with Pax8, Domotz, Hudu and Zapier, and a permission-scoped API.

What we like: Published flat per-technician pricing with unlimited endpoints, so adding client endpoints doesn’t change the bill. RMM and PSA in one platform rather than two integrated products. Importers for the platforms MSPs actually leave. A 14-day trial with no credit card that starts on the Team plan.

What we don’t like: Fewer G2 reviews than NinjaOne, Atera, Action1, ManageEngine or Datto RMM, so there’s less independent signal to check us against. Microsoft 365 management, network discovery, CIS-aligned security baselines, AI ticket summaries and automated triage all sit on Team, not Core, so compare Team against a competitor’s plan. Reporting is customizable but lacks the visual depth of a dedicated BI tool. And the Atera importer brings across customers, contacts, tickets and comments, but not invoices.

Best for: MSPs replacing Kaseya plus a separate PSA who want one platform at a price they can read, and who don’t need enterprise-scale depth.

Pricing: Core $129, Team $179 per technician per month billed annually; monthly billing available. AI features are included at no extra charge through January 2027, then run on a monthly bank of free credits. Microsoft 365 Cloud Backup is a $1.90 per user per month add-on that works on both plans. 14-day free trial, no credit card.

G2 rating: 4.5 out of 5 across 568 reviews.

4. SuperOps: Unified PSA and RMM, Now Quote-Only

SuperOps changed its pricing model in 2026. It now bills per endpoint with unlimited technicians, and both plans (Prime and Prime Plus) are quote-only, with a 150-endpoint minimum per plan.

Prime covers Windows, macOS and Linux endpoint management, patching, network monitoring and built-in ticketing and service desk. Prime Plus adds iPhone, iPad and Android management.

What we like: PSA and RMM in one product. Unlimited technicians at no extra charge, which suits MSPs with a high tech-to-endpoint ratio. No overage charges, per its pricing FAQ. A free trial before you talk to sales.

What we don’t like: No published price anymore, which takes it off the shortlist if transparency is your reason for leaving Kaseya. The switch to per-endpoint pricing reintroduces the cost-grows-with-clients problem. And the smallest G2 review base of the unified PSA and RMM platforms here.

Best for: MSPs with lean endpoint counts per technician who want a unified platform and are comfortable negotiating a quote.

Pricing: Quote only, per endpoint per month. 150-endpoint minimum per plan.

G2 rating: 4.4 out of 5 across 207 reviews.

5. ConnectWise: Enterprise Depth, Mid-Transition

Omdia calls ConnectWise the global market share leader in RMM and PSA, with almost $500 million in revenue from those products. ConnectWise Pro, announced in June 2025, bundles PSA, RMM, RPA, ScreenConnect, documentation, reporting and CPQ on one contract, licensed per end user or per endpoint.

In July 2026 ConnectWise made the ConnectWise Platform generally available, connecting PSA, RMM, remote access, SIEM, automation and AI agents in one shared system.

What we like: The deepest automation and policy control available to MSPs, an enormous integration ecosystem and an active community. A PSA that’s a genuine peer to Autotask.

What we don’t like: No published pricing; connectwise.com/pricing is a quote form. Depth comes with complexity and a longer ramp to productivity. And with a new platform rolling out, the product you buy today may not be the one you run in two years.

Best for: Larger MSPs with dedicated automation staff who need depth more than transparency.

Pricing: Quote only.

G2 rating: Automate 4.1 out of 5 across 141 reviews; ConnectWise PSA 3.9 across 590.

6. N-able N-central: The Compliance-Oriented Option

N-able now markets two RMM products: N-central UEM for complex networks at scale, and N-sight UEM for faster time to value. N-able was one of five Champions in Omdia’s 2026 RMM and PSA Leadership Matrix, alongside ConnectWise, HaloPSA, Kaseya and NinjaOne.

What we like: Endpoint, patch and vulnerability management in one console, with a CMMC compliance focus and N-able’s security portfolio (EDR, MDR and SIEM) alongside. A solid review base at 575.

What we don’t like: No published pricing; n-able.com/pricing returns a 404. No native PSA; N-able sells MSP Manager as a separate help desk. And the N-central and N-sight split means you need to be clear which product a review or quote refers to.

Best for: MSPs with compliance-heavy clients who’ll accept a quote-based relationship.

Pricing: Quote only. Free trial offered.

G2 rating: N-central 4.4 out of 5 across 575 reviews. N-sight 4.3 across 363.

7. Datto RMM: Capable, but Still Kaseya

Datto RMM is on this list with a caveat that matters: it’s a Kaseya product. It’s also where Kaseya is steering VSA 9 customers, and its kaseya.com page is now where the old VSA product URL lands.

Moving to Datto RMM keeps you in the same vendor relationship, under the same CMQ billing that applied from December 2025. It’s here because MSPs genuinely evaluate it and it’s a capable platform, not because it’s an exit. If Datto is the brand you’re leaving, see our Datto alternatives.

What we like: A well-liked platform with 810 G2 reviews. Policy-based Windows patching plus macOS and Linux update management. Built-in remote control and 200+ integrations, per Kaseya.

What we don’t like: Same vendor, same pricing opacity and same contract structure as the product you’re leaving. PSA comes via Autotask. If transparency or commitment terms are why you’re shopping, this addresses neither.

Best for: MSPs who like Kaseya’s ecosystem and want off VSA, not out of Kaseya.

Pricing: Quote only.

G2 rating: 4.5 out of 5 across 810 reviews.

8. ManageEngine Endpoint Central: Published Bands for Internal IT

Endpoint Central is a patch and endpoint management platform, priced per endpoint in published bands from 50 to 10,000 endpoints. At 500 endpoints, the cloud Professional edition is $6,545 per year (from $1.09 per endpoint per month), and on-premises Professional is $5,045 per year.

What we like: Published pricing, which most of this list lacks. Automated patching with support for third-party apps. A 30-day free trial.

What we don’t like: The standard edition is built for internal IT. MSPs need the separate Endpoint Central MSP product, and ticketing comes from other ManageEngine products such as MSP Central. EDR and ransomware protection are paid add-ons. Replacing Kaseya this way means assembling a stack, not consolidating one.

Best for: Internal IT departments, and MSPs whose main unmet need is patch depth rather than platform consolidation.

Pricing: From $1.09 per endpoint per month (cloud Professional), published in bands.

G2 rating: 4.5 out of 5 across 1,171 reviews.

9. Pulseway: Built for Working From a Phone

Pulseway’s reputation rests on its mobile experience: real remediation from a phone, not just a read-only dashboard. Its RMM covers Windows, Mac and Linux, with OS and third-party patch management, scripting and auto-remediation.

What we like: Strong mobile-first workflow and real-time alerting. Built-in patching for operating systems and third-party apps. A 4.5 rating.

What we don’t like: No published pricing; its old pricing URL now redirects to the homepage. The smallest G2 review base on this list at 125. PSA isn’t in its current product lineup.

Best for: Small MSPs and on-call teams where phone-first response is the main workflow.

Pricing: Quote only.

G2 rating: 4.5 out of 5 across 125 reviews.

10. Action1: Free Patch Management to 200 Endpoints

Action1 isn’t a Kaseya replacement. It’s here because it solves one problem for free: patch management for your first 200 endpoints, with no feature limits and no expiry. Above 200 endpoints, pricing is by quote.

What we like: A free tier that isn’t a trial. The highest G2 rating on this list, 4.9 across 1,255 reviews. Setup in minutes.

What we don’t like: Patch management only: no PSA, ticketing, billing or multi-tenant MSP business layer. Paid pricing isn’t published. Free-tier support is community-based, via Discord.

Best for: Filling a patch gap at no cost, or running alongside a platform rather than instead of one.

Pricing: Free to 200 endpoints. Above that, quote only.

G2 rating: 4.9 out of 5 across 1,255 reviews.

How to Choose Your Kaseya Alternative

Work through these in order. The first one that gives you a clear answer usually is the answer.

Name the actual complaint. Pricing opacity, contract terms, platform complexity and support lead to different shortlists. If it’s pricing, cut every quote-only vendor, which removes half this list.

Check your VSA version. If you’re on VSA 9, you’re migrating before August 31, 2027 either way. Price that move against the alternatives before your account manager schedules it.

Decide whether you’re replacing one product or two. If you run VSA plus Autotask, a platform with a native PSA replaces both. N-able and ManageEngine leave you buying a PSA separately.

Model the bill at your target size, not today’s. Per-endpoint pricing scales with client growth; per-technician pricing scales with headcount. Run both at the endpoint count you expect in two years.

Read the commitment, not just the rate. Minimum quantities (50 endpoints on Kaseya 365 Endpoint, 150 on SuperOps), term length and true-up rules are where the surprises live.

Run the migration in parallel for one billing cycle. Every importer misses something. Syncro’s Atera importer, for example, brings tickets and contacts but not invoices.

Conclusion

Kaseya’s pricing is hard to read, its billing now centers on committed minimums, and VSA 9 has an end date. For many MSPs, that combination turns a vague frustration into a decision with a deadline.

The alternatives split cleanly. NinjaOne, Atera, Syncro and ManageEngine will show you a price; the rest want a call. Within the published group, the real choice is whether your bill should grow with endpoints or with technicians.

Whichever way you go, model the bill at the size you plan to be, read the minimums before you sign, and run the migration in parallel. The platform that’s honest about price now is the one most likely to be honest about it at renewal.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Kaseya VSA cost?

Kaseya doesn’t publish it. VSA and the Kaseya 365 Endpoint bundle are quote-only, and kaseya.com/pricing returns a 404. Kaseya lists only a few prices, such as Kaseya 365 Ops at $129 per user per month on a one-year term with a three-user minimum.

What is the best Kaseya alternative for MSPs?

It depends on whether you’re replacing one product or two. NinjaOne has the largest review base and a published per-device range, plus a PSA that’s new in 2026. Atera and Syncro price per technician with unlimited endpoints and include a PSA, so they replace VSA and Autotask together.

Is Kaseya VSA being discontinued?

VSA 9 is. Kaseya has set August 31, 2027 as its end of life and names Datto RMM as the primary migration target, with VSA 10 as an alternate path. VSA 10 remains a current product.

Which Kaseya alternatives publish their pricing?

As of November 2026, NinjaOne (a per-device range), Atera, Syncro and ManageEngine Endpoint Central publish paid rates. Action1 publishes a free tier to 200 endpoints. SuperOps, ConnectWise, N-able, Datto RMM and Pulseway are quote-only.

What is the difference between per-endpoint and per-technician RMM pricing?

Per-endpoint pricing charges for every managed device, so your bill grows as clients add machines even when your team doesn’t. Per-technician pricing charges per seat with unlimited endpoints, so the more endpoints each technician manages, the lower your cost per endpoint.

Did Kaseya change its billing model?

Yes. From December 1, 2025 invoices, Datto RMM, Autotask and SaaS Protection moved from high-watermark billing to Committed Minimum Quantity (CMQ) billing. You’re billed for your contracted minimum or your actual usage, whichever is higher, so the minimum still applies when a client leaves.

Is Datto RMM a real alternative to Kaseya?

It’s an alternative to VSA, not to Kaseya. Datto RMM is a Kaseya product with the same quote-only pricing and CMQ billing, and it’s Kaseya’s primary migration target for VSA 9 customers.

Can I move my data from Kaseya’s stack to Syncro?

Yes. Syncro has a dedicated Autotask importer for PSA data, a universal agent installer for moving managed devices off any RMM, and offers contract buyout options for MSPs locked into an existing agreement.
