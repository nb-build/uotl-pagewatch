Why Deck Builders Need Better Software

Deck building is a project-based business with long sales cycles and complex material calculations. A homeowner calls for a quote, you visit the property, measure the space, discuss material options - pressure-treated pine, cedar, composite, PVC - talk about railing styles, lighting, stairs, and built-in features. Then you go back to the office, spend an hour building the estimate in a spreadsheet, and email it 2 days later. By then they've already gotten quotes from two other contractors.

The material side alone is a headache. A 400-square-foot composite deck might need 200 deck boards, 35 joists, 12 posts, 6 beams, 50 joist hangers, 15 post brackets, 1,500 screws, 80 linear feet of railing, and 4 yards of concrete for footings. Miss a line item and your margin disappears. Over-order and you're stuck with $800 in materials you can't return.

FieldFuze keeps pricing predictable. Plans are flat and monthly: Core $149, Pro $299, and Enterprise $499, each with seats included and extra seats a flat $29 a month. There is no per-technician pricing, no setup fee, and no annual contract, and card and ACH payments run in-app through Stripe with no separate FieldFuze platform fee on a paid plan. How FieldFuze Works for Deck Builders

Here's what a typical deck project looks like with FieldFuze:

Day 1 - Site Visit & Estimate

The homeowner wants a 16x20 composite deck with a built-in bench and cable railing. You measure the space, take photos of the existing yard and access points, and build the estimate on your phone - 320 sq ft of Trex Transcend decking, 2x8 pressure-treated joists, 6x6 posts, cable railing system, LED post caps, and concrete for 12 footings. Total estimate: $18,400. The homeowner signs on the spot.

Week 1 - Permits & Material Order

You submit the permit application through the city. While waiting for approval, you order materials based on your FieldFuze material list. Everything is tracked - what's ordered, what's delivered, what's on backorder. The composite decking has a 3-week lead time, so you schedule demo and framing to start once the permit is approved.

Week 2 - Demo & Footings

Permit approved. Your crew tears out the old deck on Monday, digs and pours footings on Tuesday. You schedule the footing inspection for Thursday morning. Inspector passes it - framing starts Friday. Every phase is tracked in FieldFuze with photos and time logs.

Week 3-4 - Framing, Decking, Railings

Framing goes up, decking gets installed, and the cable railing system goes in. Your crew logs materials used per day - you can see exactly how your actuals compare to your estimate. The project wraps on Friday. You take final photos, generate the invoice from the project details, and the homeowner pays through FieldFuze before your crew loads up.
