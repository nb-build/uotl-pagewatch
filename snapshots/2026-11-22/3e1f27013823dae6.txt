Quo vs Google Voice: Which Is Better for Business?

November 4, 2026

9:00 pm

Quick answer: For a US team that doesn’t already pay for Google Workspace, Quo (formerly OpenPhone) costs less: 5 users with a phone menu come to US$115 a month on Quo Business billed annually, against US$135 on Google Voice Standard plus the Workspace licence it requires. Already on Workspace, or one person on Gmail? Google Voice is cheaper. Prices as at 4 November 2026.

“Quo vs Google Voice” is usually asked by someone whose business has outgrown a Google Voice number. Google Voice is a calling add-on to Google Workspace (or, for one US user, to a personal Gmail account) with Starter, Standard and Premier tiers and Gemini note-taking on calls. Quo is a standalone per-user business phone for US and Canadian numbers, with shared numbers, phone menus on Business, and Sona, an AI agent that answers inbound calls. Below: the bill with the Workspace licence counted, A2P 10DLC texting, E911, international numbers and porting. Vendor facts come from quo.com, support.quo.com, workspace.google.com, knowledge.workspace.google.com and support.google.com, read on 4 November 2026, in US dollars.

Does Google Voice need a Google Workspace subscription?

For a team, yes. Google’s Add Voice to Google Workspace article says “Your organization needs Google Workspace before you can buy a Voice add-on subscription”, and its Compare Voice subscriptions table lists “Paid Google Workspace subscription” as the requirement for all three add-on tiers. Voice is “available as an add-on subscription to any Google Workspace edition”, so the cheapest route is Business Starter.

There is one exception, and it matters to sole traders. Google also sells Voice Starter (Standalone) at US$10 a month and Voice Standard (Standalone) at US$20 a month to a single user with a personal @gmail.com account, “U.S. only”, and its help centre says these single-user plans “don’t require a paid Workspace subscription”. Standalone Standard includes auto attendants. So the Workspace licence is a team cost, not a one-person cost.

workspace.google.com showed us Australian dollars (Business Starter A$9.90), so US-dollar figures come from an archived copy of Google’s pricing page, captured 3 November 2026: Business Starter US$7, Business Standard US$14 and Business Plus US$22 a user a month on the annual plan (“Save 16% with 1 year commitment”, billed monthly), up to 300 users. Google also advertises an introductory discount on the first three months for new customers: 20% in that capture, 50% when we re-read the live US-dollar page on 4 November 2026, when the three prices above were unchanged. Treat the discount as changeable. If you would pay for Workspace anyway (email, Drive, Meet), only the Voice line is the phone cost.

Quo Sona vs Gemini in Google Voice: what the AI does

Google Voice’s AI helps the person on the call; Quo’s Sona takes the call. Google’s product page says Voice uses AI for “blocking spam calls and transcribing voicemails”. Since 16 June 2026, according to Google’s Workspace Updates post, Gemini “take notes for me” records and transcribes a call, summarises it and emails the notes. It is “only available in English”, needs call recording switched on, and plays “This call is being recorded and captured by AI.”

Google’s pages disagree on who gets Gemini notes. The plan comparison ticks AI note-taking on Starter (Workspace add-on), Standard and Premier, and on Standard standalone. The admin article lists supported editions as Business Standard, Business Plus, Enterprise Standard and Enterprise Plus with the Voice Standard or Premier add-ons. The update post names Voice Standard and Premier add-ons and Voice Standard standalone. A fourth page, Google Voice Help’s Take notes for me article, says the feature “requires an eligible Google Workspace subscription and Voice add-ons”. Our worked bill uses the admin article, the strictest of these.

We could not find an AI agent that answers calls on any Google Voice page we read, as at 4 November 2026: the plan comparison, product page, admin help index and the Workspace Updates Voice posts for the last 90 days. The only Voice post in that window, on 8 October 2026, extended Carrier Link to 14 more countries. Google’s acceptable use policy goes further: “Do not automate our system to place phone calls or send messages automatically.”

Texting and A2P 10DLC on Quo and Google Voice

Google Voice texting is for conversations, not campaigns. Google’s texting article says paid Voice texting “is available only in the US” and free to US, Canadian and Puerto Rican numbers, and that it “is intended only for interactive conversations. It’s not intended for bulk messaging”. It lists behaviour that may be blocked: the same text to many recipients, “Sending URL links”, and many texts in a short time. Group texts top out at 8 participants, and you cannot text short codes.

Quo texting needs carrier registration first. Its carrier registration article lists a US$19.50 one-time fee paid to The Campaign Registry, a monthly fee of US$1.50 for most organisations, US$3 for non-profits or US$2 for sole proprietors, and US$15 to resubmit if rejected. “Most approvals happen within 24-48 hours”; some stay pending up to 20 days, and rarely more than 30. You cannot text US numbers on Quo’s 7-day trial. Automated texts through its API, Zapier or Make cost 1 cent a segment.

We could not find an A2P 10DLC registration step or fee on the Google Voice pages we read (plan comparison, billing, admin FAQ, texting article and acceptable use policy), as at 4 November 2026. Read that alongside the blocked-message rules above: Google Voice texting is person-to-person. For what registration involves on other platforms, see our guide to how long A2P 10DLC registration takes.

How to port a number from Google Voice to Quo

Porting a Workspace Voice number out takes a PIN from the Admin console and a request lodged with Quo, which says “most ports are completed in 3-7 business days” and ports in free (extra numbers are still US$5 a month):

If you text US numbers, Quo recommends starting 10DLC registration “before or at the same time as your port request”.

In the Google Admin console go to Apps, Google Workspace, Google Voice, Number porting, Port-out info, and copy the code (Google’s port-out article allows 4 to 10 digits). Quo’s guide says the PIN “is valid for seven days before it expires”.

Gather the address. Google says the request must match the primary location in the Admin console, which “might be different from the billing address on your invoice”; for the US it asks for “PIN and an address zip code”. Quo’s Google Voice (Paid) guide asks for a billing address that matches “EXACTLY” plus a recent bill. If they differ, use Google’s primary location and keep the bill handy.

Submit the port in Quo’s desktop or web app as an Owner or Admin.

Do not cancel Google Voice until Quo confirms the port. Google warns the new provider “could reject the port request” otherwise, and Quo notes outgoing SMS may lag up to 48 hours after transfer.

A personal (free) Google Voice number works differently: you unlock it first. Google’s port your number article says unlocking “costs $3 USD. But if your number was originally ported in from another carrier, there’s no charge.” Going the other way, Google says US ports into Google Voice take “no less than 10 business days and no more than 30, unless there are technical issues”, and toll-free numbers cannot be ported in.

Quo vs Google Voice: frequently asked questions

Is Quo better than Google Voice for a small business?

For a US team that wants shared numbers, a phone menu and an AI agent to answer calls, yes: Quo Business is US$23 a user a month billed annually, against US$20 for Google Voice Standard plus a Workspace licence from US$7. Google Voice is the better fit if you already pay for Workspace, need 911 on the line, or need numbers outside the US and Canada.

Do I need Google Workspace to use Google Voice for business?

For a team, yes: Google lists a “Paid Google Workspace subscription” as the requirement for all three add-on tiers. One US user can buy Voice Starter or Voice Standard standalone on a personal Gmail account for US$10 or US$20 a month with no Workspace, according to Google’s Compare Voice subscriptions page.

Is Google Voice cheaper than Quo?

It depends on Workspace. If you already pay for it, Voice Standard at US$20 a user is US$3 under Quo Business annual. If you do not, 5 users with a phone menu cost US$135 a month on Google (US$100 Voice plus US$35 Workspace) against US$115 on Quo Business annual. One person on Gmail pays US$10 for Voice Starter standalone against US$15 for Quo Starter annual.

Does Google Voice have an AI receptionist?

We could not find one on Google’s Voice pages as at 4 November 2026. Google Voice uses AI to block spam, transcribe voicemail and, since June 2026, take Gemini notes on calls in English. Its acceptable use policy says “Do not automate our system to place phone calls or send messages automatically.” Quo’s Sona answers inbound calls 24/7.

Can I port my Google Voice number to Quo?

Yes. For a Workspace number, get the port-out PIN in the Google Admin console under Google Voice, Number porting, and submit the port in Quo, where most ports finish in 3–7 business days. For a personal number, unlock it first; Google charges US$3, or nothing if the number was originally ported in. Keep Google Voice active until the port completes.

Does Google Voice need A2P 10DLC registration for texting?

We could not find a 10DLC registration step on the Google Voice pages we read. Google says Voice texting is “intended only for interactive conversations” and may block links and repeated texts. Quo requires registration with The Campaign Registry before texting US numbers: US$19.50 one-time and US$1.50 to US$3 a month.

Does Quo support 911 calls like Google Voice?

No. Quo says “Emergency services (911) aren’t available through Quo” and suggests keeping your carrier number for emergencies. Google lists 911 emergency support on every paid Voice tier, but its help centre says emergency calling “will not be available” during a power, internet or Google Voice outage.
