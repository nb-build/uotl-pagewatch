Best Manufacturing ERP Software for 2026: Why Epicor Kinetic Leads

For midsize manufacturers that build, configure or engineer to order, Epicor Kinetic is the ERP to choose in 2026, and pairing it with AI agents takes the keying off the team. How it compares with SAP, NetSuite, Dynamics 365, Infor SyteLine, Acumatica and Plex.
Updated November 25, 2026

Gonzalo Nuñez

Chief Technology Officer

For a midsize manufacturer that builds, configures or engineers to order, choose Epicor Kinetic. It is built for manufacturers first, it runs the job from quote to shipment in one system, and AI agents such as Fluent's already work on its screens today. The other systems below fit specific cases: SAP S/4HANA for large, global, multi-entity manufacturers; Oracle NetSuite for companies that want one cloud suite with finance at the center; Plex for high-volume, repetitive plants that want ERP and MES together; Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central and Acumatica for smaller manufacturers standardized on Microsoft 365 or wanting unlimited users; and Infor CloudSuite Industrial (SyteLine) for manufacturers already on Infor.

A disclosure: Fluent is not an ERP, but TCP, which builds Fluent, is an Epicor Platinum Partner, and our agents run deepest on Epicor Kinetic. We recommend Kinetic for what it does for manufacturers that build to order, set out below, and we describe every other system as its vendor does, including where it is the better choice.

Why Epicor Kinetic leads for midsize manufacturers

Epicor describes Kinetic as cloud ERP designed for and with manufacturers, and that is what sets it apart on this list. Most of the suites below added manufacturing to a finance or distribution core. Kinetic starts from the job.

Built around the job. Quotes, jobs, routings, planning and scheduling, quality and product configuration are in the core product, so a configured order becomes a job with its bill of materials and routing without being rekeyed.

Every way you make things. Make-to-order, make-to-stock, configure-to-order, engineer-to-order, job shop, repetitive and mixed mode run in one system, which is how most midsize manufacturers actually work.

Configured and engineered products. The Kinetic configurator and Epicor CPQ carry options, dimensions and pricing rules through to the order and the job.

A cloud with a clear path. Kinetic runs in Epicor's cloud on Microsoft Azure, and Epicor has published where on-premises feature releases end (Kinetic 2028.1), so a manufacturer planning the move knows the timeline.

AI inside the ERP. Epicor Prism adds AI agents to Kinetic that answer questions about live ERP data in plain language and flag supply and demand risks, and Kinetic's REST API lets partner agents such as Fluent's work on the same screens.

Recognized by analysts. Epicor says it was named a Leader in the 2025 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Cloud ERP for Product-Centric Enterprises, for the third year in a row.

Partners who know manufacturing. Kinetic is implemented by Epicor and by partners that specialize in manufacturers, and the partner often matters as much as the software.

Epicor Kinetic with AI agents

An ERP is only as current as what gets keyed into it. On most Kinetic sites, supplier invoices, customer purchase orders, packing slips and vendor quotes still arrive by email and are typed into Kinetic screens by hand. That is the work AI agents now take off the team, and Kinetic is ready for it: its screens, business rules and security already decide what a valid sales order, receipt or AP invoice is, so an agent that drafts into those screens inherits Epicor's own validation.

Fluent is built for that pairing. Its agents read each document, match it against Kinetic (the customer, the part numbers, the purchase order release, the receipt) and draft the transaction for the Kinetic screen it belongs to. A person approves every draft in Fluent before anything is written to Epicor, and Kinetic's business logic then runs on it as if it had been keyed. Epicor Prism answers questions about the data already in Kinetic; Fluent brings in the documents that are not there yet. Kinetic stays the system of record, and the keying goes away.

The setup is short. Metalworks connected Fluent to its Epicor in a day, and StoneAge in one 40-minute call. AI agents for Epicor Kinetic lists every screen the agents cover.

The four groups

Seven systems is a long list. They sort into four groups, and most shortlists draw from one or two of them.

Mid-market manufacturing ERPs. Epicor Kinetic, which leads this group, and Infor CloudSuite Industrial (SyteLine). Built for manufacturers first, with jobs, routings, scheduling and product configuration in the core product. They cover make-to-order, configure-to-order and engineer-to-order as well as make-to-stock and mixed mode.

Cloud business suites with manufacturing. Oracle NetSuite, Acumatica Manufacturing Edition and Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central. Broad business suites, strong in finance and distribution, with manufacturing as a module or edition. They suit small and midsize manufacturers whose production is assembly, make-to-order or lighter process work.

A plant-floor-first platform. Plex, from Rockwell Automation. A cloud platform that combines ERP with a manufacturing execution system (MES), quality management and supply chain planning, built for high-volume, repetitive plants.

Enterprise ERPs. SAP S/4HANA and Microsoft Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain Management. Broad, configurable systems for large and multi-entity manufacturers, covering discrete, process and mixed-mode production. SAP Business One is a separate SAP product for smaller companies.

Evaluation criteria

Six questions narrow a manufacturing ERP shortlist faster than any feature list.

1. Does it fit how you make things?

Start with your production mode, and be honest about the mix. A job shop quoting one-off parts needs estimating, jobs and routings that change per order. A configure-to-order manufacturer needs a configurator whose rules flow to the bill of materials and routing. An engineer-to-order shop needs engineering and production tied to a project. A process plant needs formulas, batches, co-products and lot traceability. Ask each vendor to run your hardest order, end to end, in the demo.

2. Where will it run?

Every vendor on this list is putting new development into the cloud. NetSuite and Plex are cloud only. Epicor runs Kinetic in its cloud on Microsoft Azure and has set out the path: Kinetic 2028.1, announced in January 2026, is the final on-premises feature release, which gives on-premises customers time to plan the move. Infor is moving SyteLine customers to its cloud on AWS. SAP S/4HANA, Business Central and Acumatica still offer ways to run on your own servers. If you must stay on premises, get the vendor's roadmap in writing.

3. How does it handle configured and engineered products?

If you sell options, dimensions or custom designs, the configurator decides how much work lands on engineering and order entry. Epicor (the Kinetic configurator and Epicor CPQ), Infor (Infor CPQ), SAP (variant configuration), Oracle (NetSuite CPQ) and Acumatica (Product Configurator) each offer one. Ask whether configured prices and bills of materials flow to the order and the job without rekeying, and who maintains the rules.

4. What does the shop floor see?

Ask how labor and material are recorded, how production status reaches planners, and how quality checks are captured. Plex was built around MES and quality, and that is its strongest argument. The others cover the shop floor in the core product, in a separate module or through partners; ask which, and what it costs.

5. Who implements and supports it?

Most of these ERPs are sold and implemented through partners as well as the vendor. Ask who will run your implementation, how many manufacturers like you they have taken live, and who you call after go-live. The partner often matters as much as the software.

6. How is it priced?

Ask for the pricing model before the demo: named users, concurrent users, resources or modules, and what is billed separately (configurator, MES, planning, hosting). Acumatica, for example, licenses by the resources used and offers unlimited users. Ask what an extra plant, company or currency adds.

Best for, option by option

Epicor Kinetic. Our recommendation for midsize discrete manufacturers that build to order: job shops, make-to-order, configure-to-order and engineer-to-order, often in mixed mode. Jobs, planning and scheduling, quality and product configuration are part of the product, Epicor CPQ adds visual configuration for complex products, Epicor Prism adds AI agents inside it, and agents such as Fluent's take the incoming documents off the team. It runs in Epicor's cloud on Microsoft Azure.

SAP S/4HANA. Best for large, multi-entity and global manufacturers that need discrete, process and repetitive production, variant configuration and engineer-to-order projects on one platform. It is the broadest system here and asks the most of an implementation. For smaller companies, SAP Business One covers bills of materials, production orders and MRP for discrete assembly, with partner add-ons for process manufacturing.

Oracle NetSuite. Best for growing companies that want finance, inventory, CRM and manufacturing in one cloud suite. Work orders and assemblies cover light assembly, WIP and routings add work centers and labor costing, and Advanced Manufacturing adds finite scheduling, batch management and shop floor execution. Manufacturers with heavy engineer-to-order work should test it on a real order.

Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central. Best for small and midsize manufacturers on Microsoft 365 whose production is assembly, make-to-order or make-to-stock. It runs production orders, assembly orders and MPS and MRP planning, online or on premises. Larger mixed-mode manufacturers should look at Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain Management, which runs discrete, process and lean manufacturing in one environment.

Infor CloudSuite Industrial (SyteLine). The alternative to Epicor Kinetic for discrete and mixed-mode manufacturers already on Infor, or in industries Infor names such as industrial machinery, aerospace and defense, automotive, and food and beverage. It covers make-to-order, configure-to-order and engineer-to-order, and Infor's cloud on AWS is where it is headed.

Acumatica Manufacturing Edition. Best for growing small and midsize manufacturers that want one cloud system across production, finance, inventory and CRM, with unlimited users. It covers discrete and process, make-to-stock, make-to-order, configure-to-order (Product Configurator) and engineer-to-order (Manufacturing Estimates), and offers a private cloud subscription for companies that want to host it themselves.

Plex. Best for high-volume, repetitive plants (automotive parts, metal fabrication, electronics, food and beverage) where traceability, quality and real-time production data matter most. Plex combines ERP, MES, quality and supply chain planning in one multi-tenant cloud platform, and can also run as an MES beside another ERP. Job shops and engineer-to-order manufacturers should confirm fit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best ERP for small manufacturers?

For small manufacturers, Acumatica Manufacturing Edition, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central and SAP Business One are the usual shortlist. Acumatica licenses by resources with unlimited users, Business Central fits companies on Microsoft 365, and Business One covers bills of materials, production orders and MRP for discrete assembly. Oracle NetSuite is also common when finance across several entities matters most.

Which ERP is best for engineer-to-order manufacturing?

Epicor Kinetic, for most midsize engineer-to-order manufacturers. Quotes, jobs, routings and the configurator sit in one system, so an engineered order moves from quote to job without rekeying. Infor CloudSuite Industrial (SyteLine) is the alternative for manufacturers already on Infor, SAP S/4HANA fits large global ones, and Acumatica supports engineer-to-order through Manufacturing Estimates. Ask each vendor to run one of your engineered orders, from quote through an engineering change, in the demo.

Is Epicor Kinetic still available on premises?

Yes, through Kinetic 2028.1. Epicor announced in January 2026 that 2028.1 will be the final on-premises feature release, with future innovation delivered in Epicor's cloud on Microsoft Azure. That gives on-premises teams a clear timeline to plan the move; confirm support dates with Epicor or your partner.

What is the difference between Business Central and Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain Management?

Both are Microsoft ERPs. Business Central is for small and midsize companies, with production orders, assembly orders and MRP, and runs online or on premises. Finance and Supply Chain Management is for larger and more complex manufacturers, and runs discrete, process and lean manufacturing in one mixed-mode environment.

Which manufacturing ERPs are cloud only?

Oracle NetSuite and Plex are delivered only as cloud services. Infor is moving SyteLine customers to its cloud on AWS, and Epicor's final on-premises Kinetic feature release is 2028.1. SAP S/4HANA, Dynamics 365 Business Central and Acumatica still offer ways to run on your own servers.

Should document automation affect which ERP I choose?

Plan them together. Choose the ERP for how you make things, which for most midsize manufacturers that build to order is Epicor Kinetic, and plan the document automation with it from the start. Fluent is built around Kinetic: its agents draft supplier invoices, customer purchase orders and packing slips for the Kinetic screen they belong to and route each one through approval stages, with conditions written in plain language and an approval agent that tells each approver what to check before they open it. Fluent is also offered for SAP, NetSuite, Dynamics 365, Infor, Acumatica, Plex and others. Fluent is not an ERP.

Why do midsize manufacturers choose Epicor Kinetic?

Because it is built for manufacturers first. Quotes, jobs, routings, planning and scheduling, quality and product configuration are in the core product, it covers make-to-order, configure-to-order, engineer-to-order and mixed mode in one system, and it runs in Epicor's cloud on Microsoft Azure.
