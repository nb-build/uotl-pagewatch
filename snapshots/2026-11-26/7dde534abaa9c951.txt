Epicor Alternatives for Mid-Size Manufacturers [Real Costs] 2027

Oct 29, 2026

Key Takeaways

Replacing Epicor is really two decisions: a full financial ERP with job costing and multi-entity control, or a manufacturing-first system that leaves accounting in another package. Settle that split before you compare vendors.

Five of the seven options publish at least one price on their own site. SYSPRO and Plex by Rockwell publish none on the pages checked, so their cost stays Quote-based.

A published price is the start of the budget, not the total. Acumatica's starting figure bundles implementation, while Odoo's plan price leaves implementation services out.

Manufacturing depth separates these options more than price does. Business Central documents production BOMs and versions, Odoo's app names MRP, MES, quality and shop floor, and SYSPRO's page describes quality inspections and lot traceability.

Check the data path first. If the current ERP cannot export items, BOMs, open orders and lots, both the migration and any workflow layer on top of it will stall.

TL;DR: If the plant mainly needs BOMs, inventory and shop-floor tracking, start with Katana or MRPeasy. If it needs full accounting inside a Microsoft shop, start with Business Central. If it wants one fixed SaaS bundle with unlimited users, price out Acumatica. If it wants a modular build with a partner, test Odoo. Treat SYSPRO and Plex by Rockwell as quote-only candidates that must pass a demo on your own routings.

Start with the scope decision

A manufacturing ERP is one system that holds bills of materials, production orders, inventory, purchasing and the general ledger, so a plant can follow an order from quote to shipment without re-keying data.

Epicor changed hands in 2020, when Clayton, Dubilier & Rice bought it from KKR in a deal valued at $4.7 billion, according to ERP Today (2020). Ownership is a point to raise in a renewal conversation, not a verdict on the product. A third-party profile describes Epicor Kinetic as aimed at small and mid-market manufacturers in discrete, make-to-order and mixed-mode work, according to Softwarefinder (last updated October 19, 2026).

Before you open any pricing page, answer three questions. Do you need job costing and multi-entity consolidation? Do you run discrete, make-to-order, process or mixed production? How many sites and users must share one system? Those answers decide whether a lighter manufacturing suite or a full ERP is the right category, and they tell you which options to drop early.

What each option costs on published pricing

Pricing checked October 26, 2026.

Every figure below comes from a vendor pricing page or vendor post. Where a vendor publishes no figure, the table shows Quote-based.

Essentials plan on Business Central: $80 per user per month according to Microsoft (2025). The same page lists Premium at $110 and Team Members at $8 per user per month, all paid yearly. Microsoft's May 2025 post, which set the change for November 1, 2025, lists the prior prices as $70 for Essentials and $100 for Premium, according to Microsoft (2025). The post says its figures are for information only.

On Acumatica, the APEX offer for manufacturers is a fixed-price package with a starting price under USD 2,185 per month, according to Acumatica (undated). That bundles software, implementation and support, the page says the bundle cannot be unbundled, and APEX is SaaS only. The page also says unlimited users come at no additional cost, and features outside APEX are handled case by case.

Odoo's pricing page shows the Standard plan at US$31.10 and US$38.90 per user per month, and the Custom plan at US$61.00 and US$76.20, according to Odoo (undated). The text I could read does not say which figure is annual and which is monthly, and a footnote describes a 12-month discount for initial users, so confirm the billing toggle before you budget. The same page leaves implementation services and Odoo.sh hosting for custom developments out of the plan price.

MRPeasy lists Starter at $49, Professional at $69, Enterprise at $99 and Unlimited at $149 per user per month, according to MRPeasy (undated). It offers annual billing described as one month free but shows no annual per-user figure, and from the 11th user it adds $79 per 10-user bundle.

Katana's Free plan has no user limit but caps SKUs, and its Advantage plan is quote-based. SYSPRO and Plex by Rockwell publish no price on the pages I checked. A third-party profile shows Epicor Kinetic's starting price only as custom, so those three stay Quote-based in the table.

Seven options, what each one is built for

Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central

Best fit: plants already running Microsoft 365 and Excel that want a financial ERP with production BOMs. Microsoft's training module defines a production bill of materials as the list of components a parent item requires, with quantities and units, and it covers copying a BOM and creating BOM versions.

Limitations: list prices exclude partner fees and implementation. The pricing page I opened does not list which tier includes the full production feature set, so confirm that with a partner before you compare Essentials with Premium. The Team Members seat is priced separately, so check what it can do before you count it as a full user.

Implementation: expect a partner-led project. Microsoft says its figures are for information only.

Evidence: the pricing page and the November 2025 post cited above.

Acumatica

Best fit: manufacturers that want one fixed, SaaS-only package with unlimited users and do not want to price implementation separately. Acumatica's overview calls itself "The Comprehensive Cloud ERP Solution for Manufacturers of All Sizes," which is the vendor's own claim.

Limitations: the overview page does not name BOM, MRP or shop-floor modules. Partner and reseller material describes them, so ask for a live demo of your own routing. The bundle cannot be split, and features outside APEX are handled case by case.

Implementation: built into the fixed package. The page does not say whether the post-go-live support tier (ACC-1) is in the starting figure.

Evidence: Acumatica manufacturing overview.

Odoo

Best fit: teams that want a modular build-out and have a partner to configure it. Odoo's Manufacturing app page describes one platform: "MRP + MES + PLM + Quality + Shop Floor + Maintenance."

Limitations: the first app is free, and you pay only once you install more, so the real per-user bill depends on how many apps you add. The plan price excludes implementation services, and in-app purchase credits such as SMS are separate.

Implementation: budget implementation separately, because the pricing page says the plan does not include it.

Evidence: Odoo Manufacturing app.

SYSPRO

Best fit: mid-sized manufacturers that want a cloud ERP with quality inspection and lot traceability. SYSPRO's manufacturing page says it is "helping mid-sized companies run and grow with confidence."

Limitations: the page does not spell out MRP, BOMs or shop-floor scheduling, so ask for those in the demo. Pricing is by request, and the deployment wording is cloud-first without listing an on-premise product option.

Implementation: the pricing page routes visitors to a demo request, and the implementation model is not described.

Evidence: SYSPRO manufacturing and SYSPRO pricing.

Plex by Rockwell Automation

Best fit: shops with repetitive, high-volume production that want cloud ERP tied to plant-floor execution. Rockwell's 2021 acquisition release describes Plex's core focus as "high-volume, highly repetitive discrete and process manufacturing sectors."

Limitations: that focus statement dates from 2021, so test job-shop routing hard. The March 2026 release lists finance, supply chain, production and quality modules, but it gives no company-size guidance and no pricing.

Customer scale: Plex customer count: over 700 customers in 2021 according to Rockwell Automation (2021).

Implementation: quote-based. Ask Rockwell for an integration scope covering plant systems and a migration plan.

Evidence: Plex ERP release, March 31, 2026.

MRPeasy

Best fit: smaller manufacturers that mainly need BOMs, production planning, lot traceability and shop-floor reporting. Those functions appear in the Starter plan's feature list.

Limitations: Starter lists "standard accounting," so check whether job costing, multi-entity or consolidation needs go beyond it. The Unlimited plan adds the API and webhooks, which matters if you plan integrations.

Implementation: the pricing page does not describe an implementation model, so ask about item and BOM import in the demo.

Evidence: the pricing page cited in the pricing section above.

Katana

Best fit: small make-to-order and product shops that want inventory, manufacturing orders and reporting in one tool. The pricing page says manufacturing management, including routings and insights, is included on both plans.

Limitations: the Free plan integrates only with Shopify for e-commerce, and the Advantage plan, which adds custom integrations, is quote-based.

Implementation: Advantage includes an assigned Customer Success Manager. The page does not describe implementation for Free.

Evidence: Katana manufacturing orders API reference.

Walking one illustrative shop through the numbers

Take an illustrative shop with 6 users and 45 active SKUs that wants open orders visible on the shop floor. Free plan on Katana: up to 30 SKUs according to Katana (undated), so 15 SKUs (45 minus 30) would push the shop onto the quote-based Advantage plan. Suppose a script polls the /manufacturing_orders endpoint with the status filter set to IN_PROGRESS every 15 minutes: that is 4 calls an hour, 96 calls a day, and about 2,880 calls over a 30-day month (4 × 24 × 30). Katana's developer reference lists NOT_STARTED, BLOCKED, IN_PROGRESS and DONE as the status values, and it documents location_id as an optional filter. Confirm with Katana whether API access is included in the plan you buy, because the pricing page I opened lists custom integrations only under Advantage.

Where the workflow gaps appear after go-live

Proposed workflow, not a live deployment: when a sales order is confirmed in the ERP, a proposed US Tech Automations workflow could read that order through the ERP's API or a scheduled CSV export, compare each BOM line with on-hand stock, and create a draft work order only when every component is covered. The prerequisites are read access to orders, BOMs and inventory, plus a service account limited to those objects. The output is a morning exceptions list, and a planner signs off on any draft order that shows a shortage before it reaches the floor.

A second proposed US Tech Automations build covers lot records: when a production order closes, the workflow pulls its lot numbers and inspection results, flags any lot that lacks a quality sign-off, and posts those exceptions to a QA queue. It needs export or API access to lot and inspection data, plus an agreed rule for who may clear a flag. A named QA person makes every release decision, so the workflow routes records and never releases stock on its own.

Teams that would otherwise stitch this together in Zapier, Make or n8n can do it, and those tools can keep run histories, retries, error branches and audit evidence when configured. The buyer still designs and owns observability, idempotency (so a retried order is not created twice), escalation, access controls and ongoing maintenance. A proposed build from US Tech Automations would specify those pieces before go-live: retry limits, a duplicate check on order numbers, a named escalation contact, and credentials the ERP admin can revoke.

Who this is for and when a simpler tool wins

Who this is for: an owner or operations lead whose Epicor scope is known, whose item and BOM data can be exported, and who can name the person who owns each decision.

Red flags: the current ERP has no export or API; nobody owns item and BOM data; the choice is driven only by the lowest published figure.

When NOT to use US Tech Automations: skip a workflow layer if you need only one stock sync between a web store and a spreadsheet, because a native connector or a single automation in a no-code tool is simpler. Skip it if you are replacing the ERP itself, since the ERP vendor's implementation partner has to own the data migration and the financial ledger. And skip it if the ERP exposes no API or export at all, because nothing can start until that gap closes.

Common mistakes and a decision checklist

Budget overruns are common in ERP projects: according to Panorama Consulting Group (2026), more than a quarter of organizations exceeded their project budgets, and additional technology needs were the leading cause.

Comparing license lines and skipping implementation. A low per-user figure says nothing about the migration bill.

Treating the per-user figure as the whole bill. Odoo's add-ons and the features outside Acumatica's APEX bundle sit outside the headline number.

Mixing up MRP with shop-floor execution. A planning module does not record what happened on the floor, so test both in the demo.

Migrating messy item and BOM data. Clean the item master before any vendor loads it.

Running a generic demo. Use one of your own routings and one real lot.

Decision checklist:

Do you need job costing or multi-entity consolidation? If yes, shortlist full ERPs only.

Can the current ERP export items, BOMs, open orders and lots?

Which features must sit in the base plan rather than an add-on?

Will the vendor demo your routing rather than a sample?

Is there a named partner willing to give a fixed-scope quote?

Frequently asked questions

Is Business Central a direct replacement for Epicor?

No. It is a different ERP with its own data model, so the move needs a data-mapping project and a fit-gap review with a partner before you commit.

Which Epicor alternatives publish prices you can budget from?

Business Central, Odoo, MRPeasy and Acumatica's APEX bundle publish figures, Katana publishes a free tier with quote-based paid plans, and SYSPRO and Plex by Rockwell publish none.

Is Odoo free for manufacturing?

It can cost nothing to start, because Odoo's app page says your first app is free and you pay only once you install more apps. Your real cost depends on how many apps you add.

Which option fits a job shop?

None of the pages reviewed confirms a job-shop fit, and Plex's 2021 focus on high-volume repetitive work makes it the option to test hardest for that case.

Do I need a workflow layer on top of the ERP?

Only if the ERP exposes an API or export and you have a manual handoff you can measure. Otherwise, fix the data path first.
