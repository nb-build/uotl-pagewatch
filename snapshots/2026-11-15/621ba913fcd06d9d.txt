DealerSocket

DealerSocket is a Solera dealership-technology brand with CRM, inventory and independent-dealer operating products. CRM by Solera, also called DealerSocket CRM, concerns customer engagement; iDMS concerns independent and buy-here-pay-here operations. A proposal must identify which application is being purchased before comparing the wider supplier catalog.
Official website

DealerSocket CRM and the current Solera naming

Solera’s current CRM page describes calls, texts and emails from the customer record, communication history and vehicle/equity opportunities. Its 2026 upgrade announcement separately describes conversational lead response and video in email. The older Accelerate label does not establish a separately available current package.

Inventory+, iDMS and Auto/Mate have different roles

Inventory+ addresses acquisition, appraisal, pricing and merchandising. iDMS instead supplies the operating record for independent and BHPH dealers. The separate Auto/Mate line addresses a franchise DMS purchase. Common Solera ownership does not make those product names synonyms.

For an illustrative independent-store proposal, keep a customer inquiry, vehicle choice, completed deal and later account payment separate. The CRM employee task ends at a different point from the receivable’s servicing or financial entry. Have the order identify which application owns each step.

A CRM change beside a retained DMS

For a franchise CRM proposal retaining another DMS, inspect a changed vehicle interest and the resulting customer conversation before testing the specific deal exchange. Obtain the supported object, permission and receiving action. A vendor-wide suite presentation cannot establish that particular outside-system handoff.

DealerSocket CRM versus DriveCentric Engagement Hub

Solera’s current named offer is DealerSocket CRM; the older Accelerate label is insufficient package information. DriveCentric currently calls its CRM Engagement Hub and separates agents, desking and other add-ons. Identify the customer-workflow purchase on both orders.

For a video follow-up, retain the customer’s original inquiry, the employee’s message and the opportunity stage. In each quoted configuration, have another employee continue after reassignment and inspect the received conversation. Compare the purchased video, role and DMS handoff rather than assume all functions in either brand’s catalog are included.

DealerSocket certification, product versions and partner charges

DealerSocket's certified partners page is a searchable directory that can be filtered by platform and category and shows special offers from some partners. The product matters: DealerSocket CRM, independent-dealer iDMS and a separate DMS configuration perform different work. Retain the named application, partner product and relevant release when requesting the supported connection, rather than interpret a company-level directory entry as every available handoff in the portfolio.

A certification is a statement by DealerSocket about a relationship, not a measure of how well data moves. A dealer should ask which partners are certified for its CRM version, whether the connection is bidirectional, what each partner charges for the link and whether a special offer changes the contract term. The supplier’s certification label should remain attributed to that supplier; it is not this directory’s rating of a company or a guarantee for the store’s proposed configuration.

The same partner may behave differently on DealerSocket's CRM than on its DMS, so name the product when you ask. Request the certification date and current supported version.

An earlier relationship can be useful discovery evidence without establishing new fields or a later release. Identify the customer, deal, message or other object the dealership needs and inspect it in the receiving application.

Before a first call, write down the partners you already pay for and ask DealerSocket whether each is certified for the CRM you would use, and since when. Ask what the partner charges for the connection and who supports a failing link, DealerSocket or the partner. Where another commercial arrangement affects the proposal, ask for the resulting price and term in writing rather than infer a referral fee or conflict from the existence of a partnership.

Ask for a reference at a store that uses the same partner on the same product, and ask that store how setup was organized and which receiving tasks required attention. Keep that scope with the account of its experience.

Also ask for the full list in a file you can keep. Request the certified partners by product and version with the date each was last confirmed, then retain the actual store configuration and accepted test beside the supplier’s list.

For a hypothetical CRM messaging connection, follow a corrected customer phone number and a conversation into the receiving record. Inspect assignment, history and the employee’s next action.

If the connection concerns credit or vehicle data instead, use that specific receiving task rather than reusing a messaging demonstration. An available partner offer and a completed operational test answer different purchasing questions.
