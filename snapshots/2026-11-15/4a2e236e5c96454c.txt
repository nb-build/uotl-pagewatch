Best CRM for Remodelers: 8 Top Picks in 2026

Remodeling work rarely gets lost at the estimate stage. It gets lost in the two weeks after, when a property manager stops replying, a general contractor’s bid deadline passes unnoticed, and the next job pushes the last one out of mind.

Customer relationship management software, or CRM software, built for remodelers keeps every lead, bid, proposal, and conversation in one place, so follow-up happens on schedule instead of when someone remembers.

Several of the platforms below also appear among the top ten construction CRM software tools ranked for the wider construction industry, but remodelers have specific needs that change how each one should be judged.

This guide reviews eight CRMs for remodelers in 2026, explains what separates a remodeling CRM from a generic one, and walks through how to choose.

These are the top eight CRMs remodelers should shortlist in 2026, each with the use case it fits best:

Followup CRM: Best for commercial remodelers that win work through bids.

JobNimbus: Best for roofing and exterior trades that need a mobile-first CRM.

Houzz Pro: Best for design-led remodelers sourcing leads through Houzz.

What Is Remodeling CRM Software?

Remodeling CRM software is CRM software shaped around how remodelers and home improvement contractors win work.

It tracks each opportunity from the first inquiry through site visit, estimate, proposal, and signed contract, and it ties every contact, document, and email to the project it belongs to.

That matters because a remodeling sale is longer and messier than a typical sales process.

A commercial tenant improvement can involve a property manager, a general contractor, an architect, and a facilities lead before anyone signs. A residential kitchen can sit in the pipeline for months while the owner lines up budget and permits.

A system built for this keeps the full history in one record, so no one on the sales team has to reconstruct it from inboxes.

Who Uses Remodeling CRM Software?

Residential remodelers, design-build firms, custom home builders taking on renovation work, and home improvement contractors in exterior trades all run remodeling CRM software, alongside commercial renovation contractors bidding through GCs.

The common thread across that slice of the construction industry is a long sales cycle with several decision-makers and a bid or proposal at the end of it. Customer relationship management on construction projects has to hold up over months, not days.

Construction companies that sell through quick quotes, such as service plumbers, are usually better off using field service tools such as Jobber or ServiceTitan.

Remodeling CRM vs. General CRM Software

A general CRM can support complex sales, but home improvement contractors may need to configure bid stages, project records, and proposal workflows. Generic CRMs can require more setup and ongoing administration to match a remodeling sales process.

A remodeling CRM includes these workflows from the start. Check whether its default tools support formal bids, in-home appointments, or both.

Remodeling CRM vs. Construction Management Software

Construction management software runs the job after award. It covers production scheduling, daily logs, job costing, subcontractor coordination, and the client portal where owners approve selections.

A CRM covers everything before award. The two are complementary, and the best setups hand the won project from one to the other without anyone retyping it.

Some platforms on this list combine both sides, while others focus on the sale and connect to project management tools through integrations that manage projects after award.

Key Benefits of a Remodeling CRM

Here is what changes for a remodeling business once lead tracking and follow-up move out of spreadsheets and into CRM software built for construction companies.

One Customer Record for Home Improvement Contractors

Contacts, emails, site photos, and proposals stop living in personal inboxes and start living on the opportunity, which is where customer management belongs.

When a salesperson leaves, or an estimator is out for a week, the deal history stays with the company, and the next person picks up exactly where it left off.

Fewer Missed Bids and Follow-Ups

Every bid gets a due date the system tracks, and every follow-up becomes a task with an owner. A deadline buried in an email thread no longer costs the company a job it could have won. Automated follow-up sequences cover the quiet weeks when a prospect goes silent.

Sales Reporting Construction Firms Can Plan From

Managers see every open opportunity, its stage, and its value in one sales pipeline view. Win and loss reports show which lead sources and project types pay off, which turns forecasting into reading a dashboard instead of polling each salesperson.

Less Manual Data Entry

Automatic email logging and mobile access mean activity gets recorded as it happens, from the truck or the job site. That removes the end-of-day typing that makes most teams abandon CRM software within a few months.

What Remodeling CRM Software Should Cover After the Sale

Remodeling CRM software earns its keep before award, but the best platforms also hand clean data to whoever runs the job. Here is what to check on the project side, whether the CRM helps manage projects itself or hands them off.

Compare these capabilities with your existing project processes, since each platform structures project workflows differently.

Job Scheduling and Time Tracking

Once a bid is won, someone has to schedule crews, order materials, and keep multiple crews and multiple trades from landing on site the same morning.

Builder Prime, JobNimbus, and Leap include job scheduling with crew calendars, and some add time tracking so labor hours flow into job costing without a separate app.

Followup CRM stays focused on the sale and pushes the won bid to Procore or the ERP, so construction companies that already run job scheduling elsewhere keep one system of record instead of two.

Document Management and Project Details

Plans, revised drawings, site photos, and signed proposals should attach to the opportunity, then travel with it when the job starts. Good document management means a project manager opens the record and finds every project detail the estimator collected.

Followup CRM links each document to the contact and project through its Outlook and Gmail integration, so project details reach the field without anyone dragging files between folders.

Project Budgets and Financial Management

The approved estimate provides a starting point for project budgets. Detailed job costing then compares labor and materials against that budget, so profitability shows up during the project rather than at the final invoice.

Buildertrend, Builder Prime, and Houzz Pro handle this inside the platform and can process payments from the field.

Followup CRM relies on two-way integration with Foundation, Vista, Sage, and QuickBooks Online, which suits construction companies that want financial management to stay in accounting.

Task Management and Collaboration Tools

Routine tasks such as follow-up calls, permit submittals, and selection deadlines need an owner and a date.

Task management with automated reminders keeps those off sticky notes, and collaboration tools such as shared notes and client portals enhance communication between the office, the field, and the homeowner.

A platform that carries the task list through project completion makes it easier to manage jobs without a second tool, as long as customer management and bid tracking do not get buried under production features.

How to Choose a CRM for Your Remodeling Business

For construction businesses, the right CRM depends on how work is won and who will use it every day.

A two-person kitchen and bath shop and a 40-person commercial renovation firm weigh these steps differently, so work through them before booking demos.

Map Your Sales Process Before Comparing Features

Write down how a lead becomes a signed job today. Where do leads come from, who qualifies them, how many bids go out each month, and who tracks the deadlines? That map is the specification the CRM has to fit.

A remodeler selling through formal bids to GCs and property managers needs bid stages, a shared calendar, and proposal tools built in. One selling through in-home appointments needs appointment routing and point-of-sale contracts.

Choosing software before mapping the process is how firms end up rebuilding their sales process around the tool.

Compare Setup Requirements

Compare construction CRM software and generic CRM software using the same sample opportunity. Ask each vendor to demonstrate lead intake, an estimate or bid, and a scheduled follow-up.

Confirm which steps work immediately, which require configuration, and who maintains those settings after launch.

Confirm Accounting and Estimating Integrations

The CRM should talk to the systems that run the rest of the company. Confirm the specific accounting or ERP platform, estimating tool, and email system you depend on, not the category.

Without those connections, the office re-enters the same project data in two or three places, and the CRM becomes one more silo.

Check the Mobile App Against Field Reality

Remodeling sales happen on site walks and in GC offices. A CRM that only works at a desk gets updated at the end of the day, if at all.

A construction CRM mobile app should cover the actions the field team takes, not a subset of desktop features. Run the free trial from a phone on a real site walk, not from a laptop in the office.

Ask About Onboarding and Ongoing Support

Consistent software adoption depends on clear responsibilities, practical training, and ongoing support. An intuitive interface helps, but so does a vendor that runs structured onboarding and answers questions after launch.

Ask what the free trial or demo period includes, and whether the vendor imports existing contacts during setup. Have new users complete routine tasks during the evaluation to identify a steep learning curve before rollout.

Small businesses should consider what they will need as they grow. Check whether pricing covers unlimited users or charges for each additional seat.

A scalable platform adds users and project types without a rebuild, and not all the features on a demo list get used, so weigh the ones your team will touch weekly.

FAQs About Best CRMs for Remodelers

What is the best CRM for remodelers?

It depends on how the remodeler sells.

Commercial remodelers that win work through bids, estimates, and proposals to general contractors and property managers get the most from Followup CRM, because its bid calendar, pipeline stages, and proposal tools match that process without custom setup.

Remodelers selling through in-home appointments have different needs, and a construction-specific system still beats a generic one for home improvement contractors in the field, integrations, and reporting that matter on a job.

What are the CRM systems used in the real estate field?

Real estate CRM software is built for agents and brokers.

They track listings, showings, buyer inquiries, and commission splits, and they are organized around property transactions rather than construction projects. A remodeler who adopts one ends up without bid tracking, proposal tools, or project-linked records.

Followup CRM covers the remodeler’s side of the relationship, which is the bid, the proposal, and the follow-up with the property owner or GC until the job is signed.

What is the best software for construction management?

Construction management software runs the job after award, with schedules, daily logs, change orders, and job costing. It is a separate category from a construction CRM, which handles everything before award.

The most useful setup pairs the two. Followup CRM manages leads, bids, and proposals, then hands the won project to accounting and ERP systems such as Foundation, Vista, and Sage through two-way integration.

Is there a free CRM for remodelers?

A free CRM, or a basic CRM with a free plan, can work for a one-person shop that only needs to track leads and contacts. Most remodeling businesses outgrow one as soon as bids, proposals, and project workflows need to live on the same record.
